# Pack members

A pack starts as yours alone. **Members** are other accounts you let work on
it — a builder doing your models, a friend drawing block textures, a client
watching without touching anything.

Members belong to **one pack**, not to your account. Adding somebody to your
castle pack doesn't put them anywhere near your other packs, and there is
nothing to set up before you start: open a pack, hit the **people icon** next
to Edit, and the Members page is the whole feature.

<Callout type="note">
  A pack can be shared with **five people** besides you. An invitation that
  hasn't been answered yet holds one of those places — otherwise you could
  invite thirty and have five win a race none of them knew about. Withdrawing
  an invitation, or removing somebody, frees the place immediately.
</Callout>

## Adding somebody

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the pack's Members page">
    The people icon in the pack header, beside **Edit** and **Export**. Only
    the pack's owner sees it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add them by Minecraft name or by email">
    **Minecraft name** finds an account that has verified that name by running
    `/permlink` on play.resourcepack.ai — see
    [Our test server](/testing/test-server). It's the quickest route, and it's
    the name and skin the list shows them under afterwards.

    **Email** works for everybody else, including people who don't have a
    resourcepack.ai account yet — they can create one from the invitation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose what they can reach">
    **Editor** is the whole pack. **Viewer** can open it and change nothing.
    **Custom** is the two lists below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it">
    They get an email, plus a Discord DM and a line of in-game chat if
    they've turned those on. Invitations last **fourteen days** and can be
    withdrawn from the same page at any time.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="note">
  Inviting by email always says "invitation sent", whether or not that address
  already has an account here. That's deliberate — otherwise the box would
  tell anybody who typed an address whether its owner has a resourcepack.ai
  account. Nobody's email address is ever shown to another member either;
  members appear as a Minecraft name and a head.
</Callout>

## What a member can do

Two independent choices.

**Which parts of the pack** — the same five as the tabs on the pack page:
Models, Blocks, Items, GUIs and Icons. A member granted Models alone sees a
pack with one tab in it; the others aren't greyed out, they aren't there.

**What they can do in them:**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Edit" icon="brush">
    Create, edit, rename and delete assets by hand.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Generate with AI" icon="sparkles">
    Run generation and AI edits. Spends credits — see below.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sync & test in game" icon="server">
    Push the pack to a Minecraft client and run give commands.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Export" icon="download">
    Download the pack as a `.zip`, or export single models.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The two are independent on purpose. "Can add AI models but can't delete my
work" is a real arrangement, and so is the reverse.

### What stays yours

Nothing a member is given touches the pack itself. Renaming it, deleting it,
duplicating it, changing its banner, moving assets to another pack, choosing
whose credits it spends, and managing members are all the **owner's**, and
there's no way to grant them.

## Whose credits get spent

A pack has one setting for this, in **Edit pack**:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Use my credits" icon="wallet">
    The default. Anything a member generates comes out of your balance —
    you're funding their work.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Everyone uses their own" icon="users">
    Members spend their own AI Credits and run out on their own. Yours stay
    untouched.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Either way, your own generations are charged to you, and your billing history
records **who ran** anything a member spent your credits on.

<Callout type="note">
  Members need their own access to resourcepack.ai while it's in early access.
  Being invited gets you into somebody's pack; it isn't a way into the product.
</Callout>

## Testing as a member

Sync works exactly as it does for the owner, and it sends the pack to **the
person who pressed it** — your own permalinked account, or your own server.

That last part matters if you run the plugin: a pairing code is claimed by one
player, so **each member gets their own `/link` code for the pack**. Open the
Sync menu and it hands you yours. Using the owner's would take their link off
them. See [Your own server](/testing/your-server).

Removing somebody ends this too — their code stops working the moment they
lose access.

## Hearing about what they do

The **Notifications** button on the Members page decides what you're told
about, and where. It reuses the three channels from
[Generation notifications](/notifications) — email, a Discord DM, a line of
chat in game — and you can set it per pack or override it for one person.

Things worth hearing about: somebody adding, editing or deleting an asset,
running AI generation, syncing, linking a server, exporting the pack, or
accepting an invitation. Deletes, AI generation and people joining are on by
default; the rest aren't, because a ping for every sync is a feature you'd
switch off within a day.

<Callout type="note">
  You hear about something **as it starts**, and then it goes quiet for ten
  minutes — anything that happens in between is counted and mentioned in the
  next message. Somebody saving a model forty times is one notification, not
  forty. Members are never told about each other, and never about themselves.
</Callout>

## Removing somebody

The gear beside their name, then **Remove from pack**. They lose access
immediately, their pairing code for the pack stops working, and anything they
already made stays in the pack — it was always your pack.

To change what somebody can reach rather than remove them, the same gear opens
the same two lists you picked from when you invited them.
