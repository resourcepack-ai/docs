# Transitions

A Transition covers a player's whole screen, holds while your server
changes something underneath (a teleport, a match starting, a world loading),
then uncovers it again. It's what streamers call a stinger transition, put between two scenes,
in Minecraft: a slanted dash of colour, a cartoon iris, paint splats that
join up, blocks that pop in.

Your pack draws it, smoothly at the player's frame rate and sharp at any
window size. The server only says when.

## Making one

Press **Add asset**, then the **Transition** tab. Either:

- **Describe it** ("an ink splat for a paintball server called SPLATMC").
  Studio designs three different takes, looks at each one drawn, tidies them
  up, and opens the first in the editor with the others beside it. It costs 10
  AI Credits and takes about a minute; or
- **Pick a preset**, which is free: Diagonal Dash, Ink Splat, Cartoon Iris,
  Creeper Pixels, Golden Blinds, Diamond Wave, Nether Portal, Iron Doors,
  Arcade Checker, Paper Tear, Lava Flood, Bubble Pop, Sunburst or Night Fade; or
- **From a video**, to cut a clip of your own into one. See below.

## From a video

Pick **From a video** on the Transition tab (or **Make it from a video** in
the editor of one you already have) and open a clip. An MP4 works; a **WebM
keeps transparency**, so the world shows through around your animation as it
comes in.

The clip plays over a Minecraft screen with four marks on its timeline. Drag
them to cut it into three parts:

- **In**, from **Start** to **Covered**: plays as the screen covers, up to 3
  seconds. At Covered the screen should be fully covered.
- **Hold**, from **Covered** to **Uncovers**: loops for as long as the server
  holds the screen covered, up to 4 seconds. Leave it empty and the last frame
  of the in part stays.
- **Out**, from **Uncovers** to **End**: plays as the screen clears, up to 3
  seconds. Leave it empty and the in part plays backwards.

**Sharpness** is how many pictures each frame is cut into: Standard is 256
pixels across, Sharp twice that, Sharpest three times. Sharper looks better on
a big screen and costs about four or nine times the size. The panel shows what
it adds to the pack as you change it, measured from your clip; keep it to a few
megabytes, because every player downloads it. **Use the clip's sound** turns
the sound of the in and out parts into the transition's two sounds.

Press **Use this video** and it is saved. A video transition plays in game
exactly like any other, with the same commands.

## The editor

The transition plays over a Minecraft screen, from one scene to another, exactly
as the game draws it. Drag along the timeline under it to look at any moment,
or press Space to pause.

- **Timing**: how long the cover takes to come in, how long it holds, and how
  long it takes to go. A plugin can hold it for as long as it needs; the hold
  here is what plays when nothing says.
- **Coming in**: how the cover arrives. A **wipe** (a straight edge sweeping
  across, or two doors from both sides), an **iris**, a **clock** sweep,
  **blinds**, a wave of **shapes**, scattered **pixels**, spreading **ink**, or
  a plain **fade**. Each has a direction and its own settings, an edge
  (straight, zigzag, wave, scallop or torn), softness and a pace.
- **Going out**: the same way it came in, or another way. It either **carries
  on** (a wipe leaves out of the far side) or **goes back** (an iris opens
  again).
- **Layers**: up to four colours that chase each other in, the classic
  transition look. The front one covers the screen while it holds, and any layer
  can have a moving pattern: stripes, checker, dots, a grid, a gradient or
  noise.
- **Edge line**: a band of colour along the front layer's moving edge.
- **Logo**: words in chunky pixel capitals with an outline (up to three
  lines), or a picture you
  upload (pixel art works best, up to 256 by 256 pixels), shown while the
  screen is covered. It can pop, fade, zoom, drop or spin in, and bob, pulse
  or wobble while it holds.
- **Style**: draw the whole thing in big Minecraft pixels.
- **Sounds**: a sound event to play as it comes in and as it goes, like
  `entity.player.attack.sweep`.
- **Edit with AI**: say what to change ("faster, red and black, zigzag
  edge") and the fields change. It costs 5 AI Credits, and nothing is saved
  until you press Save.

## Playing it in game

Sync the pack (or publish it with [Distribution](/distribution)), then:

**With [RP Engine](/rp-engine)** (0.2.64 or newer, see [RP Engine: Transitions](/rp-engine/transitions)):

```
/rp transition studio:<id> [player] [hold ticks]
```

The editor's **Play in game** button plays it on you. A plugin can cover the
screen, do something, and uncover it:

```java
RPEnginePlugin engine = (RPEnginePlugin) Bukkit.getPluginManager().getPlugin("RPEngine");
engine.transitions().play(player, ContentId.parse("studio:arena").orElseThrow(), () -> player.teleport(arena));
```

`cover(...)` holds until you call `release()` on what it returns, for when you
don't know how long the wait is. Anyone running `/rp transition` needs
`rpengine.transition`, and `rpengine.transition.others` to play one on somebody else.

**With plain commands**, from a command block, a datapack or any plugin: the
editor shows the commands for each transition, ready to copy. One set plays the
whole thing (in, hold, back out); another covers the screen and a third
uncovers it, so you can do something in between.

## How it works

A transition is a title. Its one character, drawn in one of the transition's own
colours, is stretched over the whole screen by your pack's text shader, which
draws the transition there. The game fades a title in and out smoothly between
ticks, and the shader reads how far through the fade it is, which is why it
plays at the player's full frame rate even though the server only works in
ticks. A player without your pack sees a small speck where the title would be.

It draws over the world and the hotbar, and under chat. Hiding the HUD (F1)
hides it too, as it hides every title.
