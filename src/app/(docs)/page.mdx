# ResourcePack AI

Build Minecraft resource packs in a browser — describe a block, a model or an
icon in plain English, edit what comes back pixel by pixel, then walk around
inside it on a real server before anyone else sees it.

## How the pieces fit

Most of your time is spent in the first row. The rest exists so that what you
make can be looked at in Minecraft in seconds rather than after a zip, an
upload and a reconnect.

| Piece | Where it runs | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Studio** | [studio.resourcepack.ai](https://studio.resourcepack.ai) | Your packs, the editors, and AI generation |
| **Test server** | `play.resourcepack.ai` | A lobby and a private world to try a pack in |
| **Plugin** | Your own Minecraft server | Receives packs and model items pushed from studio |

Nothing here replaces Minecraft's own pack format. What you export is an
ordinary resource pack — same folders, same JSON, same PNGs — so it works
anywhere a pack works, with or without us.

Models can also be **animated** — keyframed in the editor, and played back in
game on triggers like right-click or a player walking near. That part needs
our plugin, since vanilla Minecraft has no notion of an animated block model.
See [Animations](/editors/animations).

## Seeing it in Minecraft

Two routes, for two different jobs:

- **Our test server** (`play.resourcepack.ai`) gives you a lobby and a private
  sandbox world that's kept between visits. Nothing to install. Best for
  "does this texture actually read at three blocks away".
- **Your own server** runs our plugin. A player runs `/link` with a code from
  studio, and from then on you can push the pack — or hand them a specific
  model item — directly to that player. Best for showing a build team, or
  testing against your real world.

<Callout type="note">
  Both routes serve the same pack. If something looks right in the studio
  preview but wrong in game, it's nearly always the model or its texture
  mapping rather than a difference between the two servers.
</Callout>
