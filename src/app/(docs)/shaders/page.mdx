# Shaders

A shader asset is drawn by a small program in your resource pack rather than
by a flat picture. Shaders come in two kinds, picked with the **2D / 3D**
switch on the Shader tab:

![Shader objects made in Studio: a black hole, a ringed planet, a galaxy in a globe and a fireball](/images/shaders.webp "plain")

- **2D: shapes over the screen (HUD-style).** Most of this page.
- **3D: objects in the world.** A black hole, a soap bubble, a ball of fire:
  things a block model can't be. See [3D objects](#3d-objects).

Two more live beside them, with tabs of their own in **Add asset** and pages of
their own here: [Text Effects](/text-effects), which make text wave, burn or
shine, and [VFX](/vfx), spell effects drawn in the world.

A 2D shader object is a piece of HUD you design yourself: a health bar in the
corner, a nameplate at the top of the screen, a badge beside the hotbar. You
draw it, place it anywhere on screen, and it stays put at every window size and
GUI scale.

Some of it can **change while people play**. A label can read `{health}`, a bar
can fill as a value rises and falls, and a player head shows the face of
whoever is looking at it.

<Callout type="warning" title="A 2D shader object needs RP Engine">
  The art is in your resource pack, but something on the server has to put it
  on a player's screen and fill in the live values. That is [RP
  Engine](/rp-engine). Without it the pack loads and nothing appears.
</Callout>

## How it works

A server can't draw a picture on somebody's screen. What it can do is send a
line of text to the **action bar** or the **boss bar**, and a resource pack can
make a character in a font look like anything.

So your object ships as special characters in the pack's font. RP Engine sends
them to the action bar or boss bar, and a custom text shader in the pack spots
them by their exact colour and moves them to wherever you placed the object.
Everything else the game draws as text is left alone.

That is also where the limits come from, and they are covered in
[Limits](#limits) below.

## Making one

Open a pack, press **Add asset** and pick the **Shader** tab.

- **Blank** gives you an empty object to build yourself.
- **Generate** lays one out from a description, such as "health, hunger and
  level in the bottom left". What comes back is ordinary elements you can keep
  editing, not a flat picture.

## The editor

It is laid out like the GUI editor: tools down the left, the **Elements** list
beside them, the canvas in the middle, and settings on the right.

### What an object is made of

Everything sits in the Elements list under **Canvas**, and the top of the list
is drawn on top. There are three kinds of thing, and the game always draws them
in the same order:

1. **Components**, on top: labels, player heads, progress bars and pictures.
   These are placed and edited as objects, and the first three can change live.
2. **Paint**, in the middle: freehand pixels on layers, drawn with the tools on
   the left.
3. **Shapes**, at the bottom: rectangles, circles, triangles and polygons,
   drawn as smooth maths so they stay crisp at any GUI scale.

Use the arrows on a row to move it up or down. Each kind moves within its own
group: components among components, paint layers among paint layers, shapes
among shapes. An arrow at the edge of a group is greyed out, because the game
always draws shapes, then paint, then components.

### Painting

Pick a brush on the left (pencil, eraser, fill, colour picker, line,
rectangle, ellipse or pixel text) and draw on the canvas. It draws on the paint
layer selected in the list, and if there are none, picking a brush makes one.

Paint layers can be hidden, renamed (double-click), reordered, duplicated,
merged down, cleared or deleted. With the **Select** tool, dragging on paint
picks up that layer and moves it.

Pixel text stamped with the brush is ordinary pixels. For text that changes in
game, add a **Label** instead.

### Components

Add them with the **+** button beside Elements.

- **Label**: a line of Minecraft text. It can contain
  [placeholders](#live-values) like `{player}` or `{health}`.
- **Player head**: the face of the player looking at it. In the editor, and in
  any exported or distributed pack, it is Steve. A pack sent with **Sync** bakes
  in the real faces of the players it is sent to.
- **Progress bar**: fills from a live value, either solid or in segments, and
  can change colour as the value passes thresholds you set. Tick **Glow** to
  give it a soft halo in any colour; like a shape, the glow stops at the
  canvas edge, so leave room around the bar.
- **Picture**: an image you upload or have drawn, placed and sized like
  everything else. Pictures are shared across the pack, so one upload can be
  used by any number of objects.

Drag components on the canvas to move them, or nudge the selected one with the
arrow keys (hold Shift for 10 pixels).

### Shapes

**Rectangle**, **Circle**, **Triangle** and **Polygon**. A rectangle's
**Corners** setting rounds it, and a circle's **Outline** setting turns it into
a ring. Drag the handles on the canvas to resize.

Shapes combine with each other. **Add** draws on top, **Cut** removes a shape
from what is beneath it, **Keep overlap** keeps only where both exist, and
**Blend** joins two shapes with a smooth curve. Because a Cut removes what is
*beneath* it, moving a shape up or down changes what gets cut.

### The canvas

Select **Canvas** in the list to set the object's size, where it goes on screen
and what shows it.

- **Size**: up to 1024 by 1024, in steps of 8. Paint and shapes are clipped to
  the canvas; labels, heads and bars are not, and can sit outside it. Shrinking
  the canvas hides paint outside it rather than deleting it.
- **Where on screen**: snap it to a corner, an edge or the middle, then nudge it
  by pixels. Snapping follows the player's window and GUI scale, so a corner
  stays a corner on every screen. **Reposition on screen** lets you drag it
  around a full game screen instead.
- **Action bar or boss bar**: which of the two the object is sent through. The
  action bar fades after a few seconds, so RP Engine keeps re-sending it. The
  boss bar stays until it is hidden, and shows and hides instantly.

**Preview**, in the header, shows the object on a game screen at the size it
will really be.

### Full screen

A canvas has a fixed size, but a player's screen doesn't: at the default GUI
scale a 1080p monitor is 480 by 270 GUI pixels, and at GUI scale 1 it is 1920
by 1080. For something that should cover the whole screen, like a tint, a
vignette, a frame around the edges or a HUD with a piece in every corner, tick
**Fill the whole screen** on the Canvas row.

The object then covers every player's screen at any size, and each element is
**pinned** to one of nine points of it: a corner, the middle of an edge, or the
centre. Its X and Y are measured from that point to the same point of the
element, so a bar pinned bottom-right at −4, −4 always sits four pixels in from
the bottom-right corner. Pick the point in the element's **On the screen**
settings.

- **Labels** line up on their point to match: pinned right, the words end
  there and grow leftward as the value changes; pinned to the middle, they
  centre on it.
- **Shapes** can also **Stretch with the screen**. A stretched shape is sized on
  a 640 by 360 screen and scales with the real one, so a rectangle as wide as
  that is as wide as any screen. Round shapes scale by the shorter side and stay
  round.
- **Paint** keeps a box of its own, set under **Paint area**, which is placed
  on the screen like a normal canvas.

The buttons above the canvas show the object on four common screen sizes, so
you can check that everything keeps its place.

Unticking it turns the object back into a canvas, with everything where it was.

## Live values

A label or a progress bar can read a value that changes per player. Write it in
curly braces, like `{health}`.

| Placeholder | What it is |
|---|---|
| `{player}` | Their name |
| `{displayname}` | Their name with any prefix a plugin gave it |
| `{health}`, `{health_max}`, `{health_percent}` | Health |
| `{food}` | Hunger, out of 20 |
| `{level}`, `{xp}` | Experience level, and progress to the next as a percentage |
| `{xp_points}`, `{xp_needed}` | Experience points into this level, and how many the level takes |
| `{air}` | Seconds of breath left underwater |
| `{ping}` | Latency in milliseconds |
| `{world}`, `{x}`, `{y}`, `{z}`, `{direction}` | Where they are |
| `{time}`, `{day}` | The world's clock, and how many days it has run |
| `{gamemode}` | Survival, Creative and so on |
| `{online}`, `{max_online}` | Players online, and the server's cap |
| `{uuid}` | Their account UUID |

A progress bar fills from a value and a maximum: `{health}` out of
`{health_max}`, `{food}` out of 20, `{xp}` out of 100, `{air}` out of 15.

**A plugin can publish any value it likes** under any name, and a label or bar
can read it the same way. See [the RP Engine API](/rp-engine/api#hud-overlays)
for `overlays().set(player, "stamina", "40")`.

## Changing it from code

Every label, head, picture and progress bar has a **From code** section at the
bottom of its settings. Each field reads a value the same way a `{name}` does,
so a plugin, the built-in values above or PlaceholderAPI can drive it, per
player.

- **Show when** hides the piece while a condition is false, for example
  `{rank} == "vip"`, `{mana} > 10` or `perm:hud.extra`, joined with `&&` and
  `||`. It is the same language as a dialog's Show when. Nothing else moves
  when a piece is hidden.
- **Other colours** gives a label, a picture's tint or a bar's fill named
  colours, such as `poisoned` or `frozen`, and the value that picks one. Set
  that value to a name and the piece switches; anything else draws its own
  colour. On a bar, a named colour wins over the colour-by-value thresholds.
- **Length from** sets a progress bar's length in pixels from a value. A bar
  that is always full is a box your code can resize.
- **Thresholds from** moves a colour threshold from a value, as a percentage.

```java
engine.overlays().set(player, "status", "poisoned");
engine.overlays().set(player, "mana_len", "120");
```

Shapes and paint are part of the pack, so their size and colour can't change
while the game is running. Colours are picked from the ones you set in Studio;
code can't make up a new one. Each named colour costs one of the object's 32
pieces.

## Showing it in game

An object does nothing until something shows it. There are three ways.

- **A rule**, set on the Canvas. **Always** keeps it on screen while the player
  is online, **On join** shows it once as they join, **While crouching** and
  **While holding** an item show it only while that is true. Any rule can also
  require a permission.
- **A command**: `/rp shader studio:<id> [player]` shows it, and
  `/rp shader clear` takes them all off. `/rp shaders` lists every shader on the
  server. The full command is under **In game** on the Canvas, the first thing
  the editor shows, and the copy button on the object's card on the pack page
  copies it too. The id comes from the name the object was created with, so
  renaming it later does not change it. These need the `rpengine.shader`
  permissions, which are op by default.
- **A plugin**: `overlays().show(player, "studio:<id>")` and
  `overlays().hide(...)`. See [the RP Engine API](/rp-engine/api#hud-overlays).

When you're testing, **Sync** in the editor pushes the pack to you and puts the
object on your screen straight away. For everyone on your server, serve the pack
with [Distribution](/distribution), or drop its exported `.zip` into RP Engine's
`content/` folder ([Adding a resource pack](/rp-engine/resource-packs#a-pack-from-resourcepack-ai-studio)).
Both need RP Engine 0.2.38 or newer.

## Limits

- **32 pieces per object.** Each label, player head and picture takes one. A
  progress bar takes two, plus one for each colour threshold, and each named
  colour (see Changing it from code) takes one more. Paint takes one
  per 256-pixel tile of drawing, so a small drawing is one. The status bar at
  the bottom of the editor counts them, and anything past 32 isn't drawn.
- **Paint is pixel art.** It is drawn one pixel per GUI pixel, so it looks
  blocky if the canvas is large, where shapes stay smooth.
- **A full-screen object draws over the hotbar and crosshair**, and under chat
  and titles, whichever bar it is sent through. A tint over the whole screen
  tints the hotbar too. Its shapes are worked out for every pixel of the screen,
  every frame, so a handful is fine and fifty is worth thinking twice about.
- **Only players who have your pack see it.** RP Engine only sends an object to
  players holding the pack it came from, so nobody else gets a row of
  missing-character boxes.
- **Minecraft 1.19.4 to 26.2.** The text shader is rebuilt for each version the
  pack might be loaded on. 26.3 isn't supported yet.
- **Not in the ItemsAdder export.** That format has to leave the pack's font to
  ItemsAdder, and a shader object is drawn through it.

## 3D objects

A 3D object is something that sits in the world and looks like nothing a cube
model can make: a swirling portal, a planet, a glowing orb, a black hole with
a lensed disk. It is drawn by a shader in your pack, worked out fresh for every
pixel, so it is smooth and round from every side and can move.

### Making one

Press **Add asset**, pick the **Shader** tab, switch to **3D** and describe it,
for example "a slowly spinning plasma orb with a purple core and crackling
white arcs". A 3D object is always generated: there is nothing to draw by hand,
so **Blank** isn't offered. It takes a few minutes.

Things with glow, smoke, energy, glass or motion work best. One centred object
works better than a whole scene.

### The object's page

There is no editor. The page is the object running live, and three things to do
with it.

- **Look at it.** Drag to orbit and scroll to zoom. The floor squares are
  blocks, so you can judge its size.
- **Change it.** Write what to change ("make the glow bluer", "spin it
  slower", "add a second ring") and press **Change with AI**. The object keeps
  what it was until the change is done, and keeps it if the change fails.
- **Place it.** See below.

**Cost to draw** says how hard the object is on a player's graphics card:
Light, Moderate or Heavy. Several Heavy objects on screen at once can cost
frame rate on a slow machine.

**Show the shader code** shows what was written, for reading. It can't be
edited: one line that doesn't compile would stop the whole pack loading.

### Placing it

Set the **Size** (from a quarter of a block to 64 blocks across), then either:

- **Copy /summon** and run the command where you stand, with the pack applied.
  **No plugin is needed**: it is a plain vanilla command, so it works on any
  server or in single player. The object appears above you and always
  faces the camera.
- **Place in game** puts it in front of you on the server the pack is
  [synced](/testing/plugin) to, at its stored size. Sync the pack first.

Once placed it behaves like any entity: it stays where it is, blocks in front
of it hide it, and killing it removes it.

### Driving it from a server

With [RP Engine](/rp-engine) **0.2.10 or newer**, a server can control each
placed object: switch it between the looks (states) its code defines, such as
a portal opening and closing, and move, turn and resize it smoothly. Sequences
of those steps can be written in RP Engine's `objects/` content folder.

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `/rp objects` | Lists the objects the server has |
| `/rp object spawn <id> [size] [x y z]` | Places one in front of you, or at those coordinates |
| `/rp object state <name>`, `replay <name>` | Puts the nearest object into a state, or restarts it |
| `/rp object play <sequence>`, `stop` | Runs or stops a sequence on the nearest object |
| `/rp object move`, `turn`, `size`, `seed` | Moves, turns or resizes the nearest object |
| `/rp object remove [all]` | Takes it away |

Each verb has its own permission, `rpengine.object.<verb>`, all granted by
`rpengine.object`, and every verb works from a command block. A driven object
needs a 26.2 pack and a server on Minecraft 1.21.4 or newer. See
[3D objects](/rp-engine/objects) for sequences and the full command reference.

### Bubbles, glass and black holes

Some objects bend the world behind them: a bubble refracts it, and a black hole
pulls it into a curve. That needs a **26.2** pack and **Improved Transparency**
turned on in the player's Video Settings. Without it, a bubble shows as a faint
outline. On 26.2 with Improved Transparency, blocks that cut through any 3D
object also get a bright edge where they meet it.

### 3D limits

- **What it draws is all it can change.** Apart from the bending above, it
  can't distort the world behind it, and it casts no shadow.
- **Fog doesn't reach it**, so a distant object stays as bright as a near one.
- **A summoned object always faces the camera.** Only one placed and driven by
  RP Engine can be turned.
- **It needs your pack.** Players without it don't see the object.
- **Not in the Bedrock or ItemsAdder exports.**
