# Vehicles

A vehicle is a **model that has been given a job**. The model is still a
model — the same cubes, textures and animations, edited in the same editor,
listed in the same place — and converting it changes nothing about it. What
you add is everything a model can't say on its own: where people sit, how it
handles, which of its animations plays when, what it sounds like, and what
comes out of its exhaust.

<Callout type="note" title="One model, one job">
  A model is geometry; a vehicle is that geometry plus a definition the
  editor keeps beside it. Convert a model and the model page gains a
  **Vehicle** card; revert it and the page loses the card but keeps the
  settings, so converting it again picks up where you left off. Nothing about
  the cubes is touched either way.
</Callout>

## Making one

1. **Make the model.** Generate it, upload it or build it, exactly as on the
   [Models](/models) page: a go-kart, a rowing boat, a biplane. Build it
   facing the front of the block (**+Z**), because that is the way it drives
   and the way its driver looks. Land, water and air all start life as an
   ordinary model.
2. **Convert it.** On the model's page, the **Vehicle** button in the header
   opens the same dialog as right-clicking the model's card and choosing
   **Convert to…**. Converting drops you straight into the vehicle editor,
   because a vehicle with no driver seat does nothing at all in game.
3. **Give it a driver**, then as much of the rest as it needs.

## The vehicle editor

The model sits in the viewport with its seats, hitbox and effects drawn over
it; everything else is in the panels beside it.

### Moves through

Land, water or air. The choice decides the physics:

| Medium | In game |
| --- | --- |
| **Land** | Falls, drives on blocks, steps up one like a player |
| **Water** | Floats on water; on land it drags |
| **Air** | Flies once it is going fast enough, sinks when it slows |

### Handling

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Speed** | Top speed, in blocks a second. A kayak is about 6, a car 18, a plane 30 |
| **Acceleration** | How quickly it gets there, and how quickly it stops |
| **Turning speed** | Degrees a second at full lock |
| **Weight** | 1 to 100. Feel rather than physics: how it shoves mobs and how it settles |
| **Jumps** | A land vehicle that jumps on Space instead of braking — a dirt bike, a skateboard |
| **Turns on the spot** | Pivots where it stands without moving, for tracked things, hovercraft and diggers. Off for anything with wheels, a hull or wings |
| **Size** | How big the model stands, as a multiplier |
| **Hitbox** | Width, height and length in blocks: what you click to get in, and what stops it at a wall |

An air vehicle also has **Flight**: the speed it takes off at, how fast it
climbs and dives, and how fast it sinks when it has stalled.

<Callout type="tip" title="Above about 20 blocks a second, look before you ship">
  A passenger's position is broadcast twenty times a second and their client
  fills in the gaps, which stops looking right above that. The driver holds
  up better, because steering is their own camera.
</Callout>

### Riders

One driver and up to seven passengers. Each seat has:

- **Where it is**, dragged in the viewport or typed as an exact position in
  the model's own pixels, so a saddle lands on the saddle.
- **A pose**, sitting or standing. A sitting seat's point is where the
  rider's backside goes; a standing one's is where their feet go.
- **Which way it faces.** Front is the default; a passenger can face
  backwards, and a skater stands side-on.
- **Hide the rider**, for a seat somebody occupies without being drawn.
- **What they hold**: a paddle, a wheel, handlebars. Name a part of the model
  and it follows the rider's hands through every animation, so the paddle is
  wherever the arms put it.

The driver steers; passengers ride. Anybody can get in by right-clicking the
vehicle and out by sneaking.

### Animations

A vehicle's animations are the model's own, made in the editor's Animate
mode or by AI, and the **state map** says which one plays when. Each state
has a slot for the vehicle's animation and a slot for each rider, and the
rider's is an **emote**: pose it on the vehicle from here, or make one for the
state and come back.

| State | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Airborne** | Off the ground with nothing holding it up: a jump, a fall, an aircraft in flight |
| **Turning** | Swinging round faster than a nudge. Beats Moving, so a cornering vehicle plays this |
| **Reversing** | Travelling backwards |
| **Moving** | Travelling forwards |
| **Idle** | Stationary, or near enough that nobody can tell |
| **In water** | Its base is in the water and nothing above it applies. A moored boat is Idle, not this |

The list is in order of precedence: the first state that is true and has an
animation is the one that plays. A state you leave blank falls through to the
next one, so a vehicle with only **Moving** filled in plays its drive cycle
while cornering too. **Idle** is the floor: leave it blank and a still
vehicle plays nothing.

Changes of state are smooth. The engine crossfades from whatever the rig is
showing into the next cycle and joins that cycle where the rig already is,
so a wheel keeps its angle across moving, turning and reversing, and stays
where it stopped when the vehicle does.

### Animate with AI

Rather than keyframing each state by hand, **Animate with AI** does the lot:
it groups the model's cubes into bones (wheels, steering, rotor, paddle),
puts a rider in every seat, and writes one looping animation per state for
the bodywork and the people together — wheels rolling, a driver's hands on
the wheel, a passenger holding on. Pick which states to animate, and tick
**Set it up too** to have it plan the handling and the seats from the model
as well. Run it again for one state to redo just that one; the others are
kept.

<Callout type="tip" title="Generation is a starting position">
  Look at it in game, then fix what is wrong. A head that turns too far, a
  wheel that rolls the wrong way: open the animation, move the keyframe.
</Callout>

### Sounds

A sound per state, so an engine can tick over at idle and work harder
moving. Pick one from the pack or make a new one in the sound editor. In
game the sound follows the vehicle rather than playing from the spot it was
in when the state changed.

### Effects

Exhaust smoke, spray off a hull, a rotor's downwash. Each emitter has a
particle effect, where on the model it is drawn, which states it plays in,
and how much: count, spread, speed, how often, colour and size. Add as many
as the vehicle needs.

### Capes

Riders' capes are put away while they are in the vehicle, or not — a
cloak trailing off a motorbike is right, and off a go-kart it is a cape stuck
through the seat.

## Seeing it in game

A vehicle reaches a server the way a model does, and needs RP Engine on
that server:

1. **Push the pack** to a server you have paired, or get the item with the
   model page's **Give in-game** button on the test server. See
   [The plugin](/testing/plugin).
2. **Use the item** to park the vehicle where you are looking. It stays
   where you left it.
3. **Right-click to get in.** On Paper 1.21.4 and up, W and S drive, A and D
   steer, and Space is the handbrake — or the jump, for a vehicle that jumps.
   In the air, Space climbs and S descends. On older servers, right-click
   speeds up, left-click slows and reverses, and you steer by looking.
4. **Fix it from the game.** `/rp edit` opens the vehicle in this editor from
   the server's own copy; Send writes it back. See
   [Editing from the server](/rp-engine/editing).

## What it becomes

A resource pack can carry a model, but it has no way to say that the model is
a vehicle: there is no field in a model for a seat. So the vehicle rides in
the **content manifest** that goes with a pushed pack, alongside its sounds
and screens, and RP Engine reads it from there. On the server it becomes an
invisible chassis wearing your model, with an invisible seat for each rider,
and the model's animations are played on the chassis by state.

That is also why a vehicle is a Studio-and-RP-Engine thing. The plain pack
download, the Bedrock pack and the ItemsAdder and ModelEngine exports all
carry the model, because it is a model; none of them carries the job.
