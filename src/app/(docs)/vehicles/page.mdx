# Vehicles

A vehicle is a model people can ride: a go-kart, a rowing boat, a biplane.
You make the model as usual, convert it, and then say where people sit, how it
handles, which animation plays when, what it sounds like, and what comes out
of its exhaust. Converting changes nothing about the model itself. The cubes,
textures and animations are still edited in the model editor.

![A player riding a skateboard, a vehicle, in game](/images/vehicles.webp)

Vehicles are driven by [RP Engine](/testing/plugin), so they need it on the
server. Our test server has it.

## Making one

1. **Make the model.** Generate, upload or build it, as on the
   [Models](/models) page. Build it facing **+Z** (the front of the block),
   because that's the way it drives and the way the driver looks.
2. **Convert it.** On the model's page, press **Vehicle** in the header, or
   right-click the model's card and choose **Convert to…**. The vehicle
   editor opens straight away. From then on it's on the pack's **Vehicles**
   tab rather than under Models.
3. **Add a driver seat**, then as much of the rest as it needs. A vehicle
   without a driver does nothing in game.

Most vehicles are finished with [Animate with AI](#animate-with-ai), which
sets up the moving parts and the riders in one go.

## The vehicle editor

The model sits in the viewport with its seats, hitbox and effects drawn over
it. Settings are in the panels beside it, and changes save on their own.

### Moves through

| Medium | In game |
| --- | --- |
| **Land** | Falls, drives on blocks, and steps up one block like a player |
| **Water** | Floats on water; on land it drags |
| **Air** | Flies once it's going fast enough, and sinks when it slows |

A land vehicle can also be set to **Jumps**: Space hops it a block and a half
instead of being the handbrake. Use it for a dirt bike or a board.

### Handling

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Speed** | Top speed in blocks a second, 1 to 60. A kayak is about 6, a car 18, a plane 30 |
| **Acceleration** | How quickly it gets up to speed and stops |
| **Turning speed** | Degrees a second at full lock |
| **Weight** | 1 to 100. How it pushes mobs aside and settles, rather than real physics |
| **Turn on the spot** | Pivots without moving, for tanks, hovercraft and diggers. Leave it off for anything with wheels |
| **Hitbox** | Width, height and length in blocks: what you click to get in, and what stops it at a wall |

**Size** (**Drawn at**) scales the model, which is how you make a bus or a
ship bigger than the three blocks a model can be on its own. Seats and
effects scale with it; the hitbox and handling don't.

An air vehicle also has **Flight**: **Takeoff speed**, **Climb rate**, **Dive
rate**, and **Stall sink**, how fast it drops once it's too slow to fly.

<Callout type="tip" title="Test fast vehicles in game">
  Passengers' positions are only sent twenty times a second, so above about 20
  blocks a second they can look jumpy. The driver looks fine, because their
  own camera follows the steering.
</Callout>

### Riders

One driver and up to seven passengers. For each seat:

- **Where it is**: drag it in the viewport, or type an exact position in the
  model's pixels.
- **Pose**: **Sitting** puts the rider's backside on the point, **Standing**
  puts their feet there.
- **Which way it faces**: front by default. A passenger can face backwards,
  and a skater stands side-on.
- **Hide the rider**: for a seat whose occupant shouldn't be drawn.

The driver steers and passengers ride. Anyone gets in by right-clicking the
vehicle and out by sneaking. **Show capes** decides whether riders' capes stay
on while they're aboard: right for a motorbike, wrong for a go-kart.

### Animations

A vehicle plays the model's own animations, and the **state map** says which
plays when. Each state has a slot for the vehicle and a slot for each rider.
A rider's slot takes an **emote**: pose one on the vehicle from here, or make
a new one for that state.

| State | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Airborne** | Off the ground with nothing holding it up: a jump, a fall, a plane in flight |
| **Turning** | Turning harder than a nudge |
| **Reversing** | Moving backwards |
| **Moving** | Moving forwards |
| **Idle** | Standing still |
| **In water** | In water with nothing above applying. A moored boat is Idle |

The list is in order of priority: the first state that's true and has an
animation plays. A blank state falls through to the next, so a vehicle with
only **Moving** filled in plays that while turning too. If **Idle** is blank, a
still vehicle plays nothing.

Changes of state blend smoothly, and a wheel keeps its angle from one state to
the next.

### Animate with AI

**Animate with AI** does all of this for you. It groups the model's cubes into
bones (wheels, steering, rotor, paddle), puts a rider in every seat, and
writes a looping animation for each state, bodywork and people together:
wheels rolling, hands on the wheel, a passenger holding on. A part a rider
uses, like a paddle, follows their hands.

Choose the **States to animate**, and tick **Set it up too** to also have it
plan the handling and seats from the model. Run it again for one state to
redo only that one.

<Callout type="tip">
  Then look at it in game and fix what's wrong. A wheel that rolls backwards
  is one keyframe in the animation.
</Callout>

### Sounds

One sound per state, so an engine can idle quietly and work harder when
moving. Pick one from the pack, or make a new one in the sound editor. In game
the sound follows the vehicle.

### Effects

Exhaust smoke, spray off a hull, a rotor's downwash. Each effect has a
**Particle effect**, where on the model it's **Drawn at**, which states it
**Fires while**, and how much: count, spread, speed, how often, colour and
size. Add as many as you need.

### Vehicle status (crash damage)

Tick **Enable GTA-style crash damage** for vehicles that take damage. Light
knocks do little; a hard crash dents that corner of the body, can tear off
parts, makes it handle worse, and a destroyed vehicle stops responding.

- **Durability**: 1 is standard. 2 takes half the damage, 0.5 twice as much.
- **Detachable model parts**: the bones that can break off. Mark the real
  wheels, so losing one drops that corner and slows the vehicle down.
- Further switches cover whether crashes hurt the people aboard, whether a
  wreck smokes and burns or explodes, and whether water writes it off.

<Callout type="note" title="Needs the Vehicle Status addon">
  Crash damage is done by the Vehicle Status addon for RP Engine, which the
  server has to install alongside it. See the
  [addons page](https://resourcepack.ai/rp-engine/addons). Without it, the
  settings are kept but nothing takes damage.
</Callout>

## Seeing it in game

1. **Sync the pack** to a server running RP Engine, or press **Give in-game**
   on the model's page while you're on the test server.
2. **Use the item** to park the vehicle where you're looking. It stays where
   you leave it.
3. **Right-click to get in.** On Paper 1.21.4 and newer, W and S drive, A and
   D steer, and Space is the handbrake (or the jump). In the air, Space climbs
   and S descends. On older servers, right-click speeds up, left-click slows
   and reverses, and you steer by looking.
4. **Fix it from the game.** `/rp edit` opens the vehicle in this editor from
   the server's copy, and **Send** writes it back. See
   [Editing from the server](/rp-engine/editing).

## Exports

A resource pack has no way to say a model is a vehicle, so the vehicle
settings travel alongside a synced pack and RP Engine reads them there. The
plain Java and Bedrock downloads and the ModelEngine export carry the model,
but not the vehicle. The ItemsAdder, Nexo, Oraxen and CraftEngine exports
turn it into furniture you can sit in, but those plugins can't drive it.
