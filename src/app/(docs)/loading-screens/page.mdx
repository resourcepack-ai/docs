# Loading Screens

A Loading Screen restyles the screen Minecraft shows while resource packs load:
normally red, with the Mojang Studios logo and a white bar. Yours can have its
own colours, your server's name and logo, an effect like falling snow or rising
embers, and a progress bar to match.

## Making one

Press **Add asset** and pick the **Loading Screen** tab. Either:

- **Describe it** ("a spooky Halloween server called Grimfall"). Studio designs
  the colours, effect, words and bar, which costs 5 AI Credits and takes a few
  seconds; or
- **Pick a look**, which is free: Winter Night, Nether, Starfield, Firefly
  Forest, Thunderstorm, Royal Gold, Ocean, Clean, or Recoloured (Minecraft's
  own screen and logo in a colour of your choosing). The name you give it
  becomes the title on the screen.

Either way it opens in the editor.

## The editor

The screen plays live, as the game draws it: the effect moves and the bar
fills. The panel on the right has every part of it.

- **Looks**: switch to another ready-made style. Your words and picture stay.
- **Background**: one colour, a gradient from top to bottom, or a gradient
  out from the middle, with an optional glow behind the logo.
- **Effect**: none, snow, rain, stars, embers, sparkles or fireflies, in any
  colour and amount.
- **Logo**: your own (a picture, a title and a subtitle), Minecraft's own
  logo, or none. A picture is best as pixel art: up to 128 by 64 pixels and 12
  colours, and bigger ones are shrunk to fit. Titles are drawn in chunky pixel
  capitals.
- **Bar**: the frame and fill colours, solid, a gradient along the bar, or
  moving stripes, and an optional colour for its empty part.
- **Edit with AI**: say what to change ("make it purple", "add fireflies") and
  the fields change. Nothing is saved until you press Save, so you can undo it.

A pack shows one Loading Screen. If it has several, the card marked **Shown**
is the one in the pack; press **Use this** on another to switch.

## When players see it

It works on **Minecraft 26.1, 26.2 and 26.3**. Older versions see Minecraft's
own screen. On 26.1 the effect stands still, because that version gives the
loading screen no clock; it moves on 26.2 and 26.3.

A player sees it whenever the game reloads resources while your pack is
already loaded:

- pressing F3+T;
- turning packs on or off in the Resource Packs menu;
- a server sending a second pack, or leaving a server whose pack is then
  taken off.

The very first load on joining a server still shows Minecraft's red screen:
the game hasn't got your pack yet while it is loading it. The very first
screen when the game starts is Minecraft's too, for the same kind of reason.

## How it works

Minecraft reads the Mojang logo from its own files only, so a resource pack
can't simply replace it. Instead your pack overrides the two shaders the
loading screen is drawn with, recognises the background, the logo and the bar
by where they are, and paints your design over them. Everything else drawn
with those shaders is left exactly as it was. The shaders only load on the
versions above, so a newer or older Minecraft falls back to the normal screen
rather than breaking your pack.
