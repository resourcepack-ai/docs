# GUIs

A GUI asset reskins one of Minecraft's **screens**: the chest window, the
furnace, the anvil, the enchanting table. You draw a 256×256 sheet with the
screen's window sitting in the middle of it, and that sheet draws over the
screen when it opens.

<Callout type="warning" title="A GUI is draw-only">
  A pack changes how a screen looks. It cannot move a slot, add one, resize the
  window or change what any of it does. That's server code, and no Resource
  Pack has ever been able to reach it. Design against the slot positions you're
  given, not the ones you'd like.
</Callout>

## How it actually works

This one is worth understanding, because most GUIs here are **not** a texture
override and the difference shows up everywhere else. (The exception is the
player's inventory, which is one — see below.)

The sheet ships as a **font glyph**, and a plugin opens the screen with a title
made of two invisible characters: a negative-space character that walks the
cursor back to the window's top-left, then the glyph itself. Minecraft draws
the title, the title happens to be your entire image, and it lands exactly over
the window.

Two consequences follow, and both are the point:

- **Vanilla screens stay vanilla.** The files under `textures/gui/` are never
  touched, so an ordinary chest a player opens looks like an ordinary chest.
  Only a screen a plugin opens with your title gets the custom look.
- **You can draw outside the window.** The sheet is bigger than the window, and
  the margin renders too, so a banner, a border or a pair of hanging chains can
  overflow the frame. The dashed rectangle in the editor is where vanilla's
  window sits; everything outside it is yours.

## Which screens

The picker covers the screens whose title text actually renders, grouped by
what they're for:

| Group | Screens |
| --- | --- |
| **Storage** | Chest (1–6 rows), Shulker Box, Dispenser / Dropper, Hopper |
| **Crafting & smelting** | Crafting Table, Furnace, Blast Furnace, Smoker, Smithing Table, Stonecutter, Grindstone, Loom, Cartography Table |
| **Utility** | Enchanting Table, Anvil, Brewing Stand |
| **Player** | Player Inventory |

The beacon is missing on purpose: it draws no title, and with no title there's
nowhere to put an overlay.

## The player's inventory is the other kind

The **Player Inventory** in that table works differently from everything above
it, and the difference matters more than it sounds.

Nobody opens a player's own inventory for them. There is no plugin call, no
title to set, and therefore no glyph trick to play — so that screen is reskinned
the only other way a pack can reskin anything: by **replacing the vanilla
file**, `textures/gui/container/inventory.png`. Pick it in the Draw door like
any other screen and it starts from the real vanilla art, in the pixel editor,
with the same slot guides.

What replacing costs you, all three worth knowing before you draw one:

- **It applies everywhere, always.** Every inventory every player opens, on
  every server running the pack, whether or not a plugin asked for it. That is
  the opposite of the overlay's opt-in behaviour.
- **A pack holds one.** Two assets replacing the same file would both export to
  the same path, so the second one is refused with a message saying which asset
  already has it.
- **There is no margin.** The game draws the window from the file's top-left
  corner and ignores everything outside it, so unlike an overlay there is no
  overflow room — art past the window's 176×166 edge simply never appears. The
  editor's dashed rectangle sits at the sheet's corner rather than in the
  middle, which is how you can tell the two apart at a glance.

Everything else — the editor, the slot guides, export, moving it to another
pack — is identical.

## Making one

GUIs are drawn rather than generated: you start from the real vanilla window
art for the screen you picked, already centred in the sheet, and edit from
there. There's no prompt box for a whole screen yet — the AI isn't capable of
drawing one, and we're working on it. You can have it draw a single image into
a box you pick, which is a different job and one it's good at; see below.

You start from the vanilla backplate, already centred in the sheet.

## The editor works in slots, not pixels

The GUI editor is not the pixel editor the other asset kinds use, and the
difference is the point. A screen has a fixed mechanical layout — 18×18 slot
cells at coordinates the game decides — so the tools take a **slot** as their
argument rather than a marquee you have to line up by hand:

- **Slot brush** stamps a styled cell: carved (the generator's own look),
  vanilla grey, flat, or outline-only.
- **Paint** covers a whole slot in one flat colour, and defaults to vanilla's
  panel grey — so a painted slot doesn't look painted, it looks *gone*, because
  what's left matches the panel either side of it. That's how you get a 5-slot
  row on a 9-slot chest.
- **Eraser** clears a whole slot, not a smear of pixels. (It cuts a hole:
  what shows through is the world behind the screen, which is usually not what
  you want on a panel — reach for Paint instead.)
- All three take a **scope**, which is what turns a fiddly job into one click:
  the slot under the cursor, its row, its column, the whole screen /
  inventory / hotbar block, or every slot at once. There's a `Pixels` scope
  too, for free-hand touch-ups.
- **Card** and **Button** are dragged across the slots they should cover and
  snap to them, so furniture lines up with the items the game will draw.
- **Title band**, **frame** and **label** place themselves from the screen's
  own geometry.

The layers panel's **+** asks what you're adding. One of the answers is a
**drawn image**: you drag a box anywhere on the sheet — free pixels, not slots,
so it can sit over the frame or out in the margin — say what goes in it, and
it's drawn at exactly that size and lands as its own layer. Pick *Object* for
one thing on nothing (it comes back cut out) or *Scene* for something that
fills its box. It costs credits like any other image, and it's the only AI in
this editor: everything else here is drawn or composed, and free.

Hovering a slot lights it the way Minecraft does — a white wash over the
16×16 item area — and the row or group a click would touch is highlighted
before you commit to it. The player inventory and hotbar draw at a fixed
offset below the window on most screens, and they're part of the lattice too.

Cards, buttons, frames and carved cells are all derived from one **theme**
colour, seeded from the art already on the sheet. Changing it restyles what
you draw next; it doesn't repaint what's already there.

## Seeing it in game

An overlay's page carries the exact command that opens it, plus an **Open
in-game** button that runs it for you in game:

```
/gui @p chest_54 \ue000\ue001
```

Those last two characters are this GUI's own: the negative-space one, then
the glyph. They're allocated per pack, so copy the command off the page
rather than typing it.

That's our plugin's command, and it's runnable by any player rather than
being op-only, so the person who just drew the sheet can look at it. The
screen it opens is a real one, so you're judging the art against real slots
and real item stacks.

A replaced screen has no such command and doesn't need one — Sync the pack and
press <kbd>E</kbd>.

## Exporting

An overlay leaves as a PNG under `assets/minecraft/textures/font/`, plus an
entry in the pack's font provider that binds it to its character. There's no
`gui/` file in the export, which is what keeps ordinary screens ordinary.

A replaced screen (the player's inventory) leaves as the vanilla file it
replaces — `assets/minecraft/textures/gui/container/inventory.png` — and needs
no font entry, no title and no plugin. It works the moment the pack is applied.

The half that isn't in the pack is the **title**. Your own plugin has to open
the inventory with that exact string, in white, or the overlay never appears:

```java
// The title is copied from the GUI's page in the Studio.
Inventory inv = Bukkit.createInventory(null, 54, Component.text("\ue000\ue001").color(NamedTextColor.WHITE));
player.openInventory(inv);
```

White matters, because the glyph is drawn in the title's colour and a coloured title
tints your whole overlay.

<Callout type="note" title="Moving a GUI to another pack renumbers it">
  The characters are allocated per pack, so an overlay copied into a different
  pack gets new ones and its title string changes. Re-copy it from the GUI's
  page rather than reusing the old string. (A replaced screen has no characters
  to renumber — but it won't copy into a pack that already has one of its own.)
</Callout>

## On Bedrock

None of this crosses over. Bedrock's screens aren't drawn from a Java font
provider, and there's no title trick to exploit, so a GUI is Java-only. The
rest of your pack converts as normal.
