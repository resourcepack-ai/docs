# GUIs

A GUI asset gives one of Minecraft's **screens** a new look: a chest window, a
furnace, an anvil, a shop menu your server opens. You can have the AI design
the whole screen, lay it out yourself with buttons, text and icons, or paint
over the vanilla art by hand.

<Callout type="warning" title="A GUI changes the look, not the layout">
  A resource pack can't move a slot, add one, resize the window or change what
  a click does. That's server code. Design around the slots the screen already
  has. (Buttons you draw are pictures; a server plugin decides what clicking
  that slot does.)
</Callout>

Screens with real buttons and text fields, like a welcome screen or a rules
page, are [dialogs](/dialogs), which have their own page.

## Three kinds of screen

| Kind | Screens | How it reaches the game | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Overlay** | Chests, furnaces, the anvil and the others below | Drawn over the screen when a plugin opens it with this GUI's title | Only screens opened that way. Ordinary chests stay ordinary |
| **Player Inventory** | The <kbd>E</kbd> screen | Replaces the vanilla file | Every player's inventory, always |
| **Escape Menu** | The pause screen's buttons and background | Replaces the vanilla button sprites and labels | Every menu in the game |

The overlays you can pick:

| Group | Screens |
| --- | --- |
| **Storage** | Chest (1 to 6 rows), Shulker Box, Dispenser / Dropper, Hopper |
| **Crafting & Smelting** | Crafting Table, Furnace, Blast Furnace, Smoker, Smithing Table, Stonecutter, Grindstone, Loom, Cartography Table |
| **Utility** | Enchanting Table, Anvil, Brewing Stand |

The beacon isn't offered: it draws no title, so there's nowhere to put an
overlay.

### How an overlay works

Your sheet ships in the pack as a character in a font. A plugin opens the
screen with a title made of two invisible characters: one that moves the text
cursor to the window's corner, then your picture. Minecraft draws the title,
and the title is your whole screen.

Because the sheet is bigger than the window, you can draw outside it: a
banner, a frame, hanging chains. The dashed rectangle in the editor is where
vanilla's window sits.

### The Player Inventory

Nobody opens your inventory for you, so there's no title to use. Instead the
pack replaces vanilla's `inventory.png`. That means:

- It applies to every inventory, for everyone with the pack.
- A pack holds one.
- There's no margin: anything outside the 176×166 window isn't drawn.

### The Escape Menu

The pause screen has no window. Studio themes the shared button face (with its
hover and disabled states), the background, and each button's label, so
"Back to Game" can carry its own art. It's generated from one description of
the material. A few things to know:

- The button face is shared, so the title screen, options screens and world
  list get the same look.
- Each button's art replaces its words, so the art carries the wording.
- The pause screen's buttons no longer light up under the cursor.
- A pack holds one. It opens in the pixel editor, with a guide box round each
  sprite.

## Making one

Open a pack, press **Add asset** and pick **GUI**.

**Generate** takes a description and a **Layout**:

- **Planned for you**: describe the whole screen, "an underwater pirate shop",
  and the AI picks the size, title plaque, frame, buttons and layout. Choose a
  **Style**: **Painted** (illustrated, every element drawn by the image model)
  or **Blocky** (built from the editor's own pieces, which you can keep editing
  as separate layers).
- **Design it yourself**: pick the **Screen to reskin** and place the buttons
  and drawings yourself.

For the Player Inventory you choose whether to draw the slot cells and colour
them from your panel; vanilla owns the rest of the layout.

**Blank** starts from the real vanilla art for the screen, centred on the
sheet. **Upload** takes a 256×256 sheet.

## The editor

The GUI editor shows the vanilla window frame, a guide box on every slot (and
on the player's inventory and hotbar below most screens), so you can line a
panel up with the grid.

Add pieces from its add menu: **Button**, **Text**, **Formatted text**,
**Icon**, **Title plate**, **Scroll bar**, **Tabs**, **QR code**, **Upload
image**, **AI image**, **Background** and **Player head**. A player head shows
the viewer's own face and needs RP Engine on the server. Everything is a layer
you can move and edit.

`Ctrl+C` copies the selected piece and `Ctrl+V` pastes it, into this GUI or
another one in any tab. Pasting pixels copied from a texture, or an image
copied outside Studio, adds it as an image.

### Your own fonts

Words can be set in a font of your own. Under **Font** in a piece's panel (a
text, title plate, button, tab or formatted text), choose **Upload a font
(.ttf)**. The font is kept with the pack, so every GUI and dialog in it can use
it, and it appears in the Text tool too.

The words are drawn into the screen as pixels, like the built-in faces, so
nothing extra ships and no player needs the font. A pixel font is drawn exactly
at its own size, which the editor works out for you, and at 2x or 3x. Any other
font is drawn at the size you pick and looks best at 10px or more. Fonts can be
TrueType (`.ttf`) or a collection (`.ttc`), up to 2 MB; an `.otf` whose letters
are PostScript curves cannot be read, so use the font's `.ttf` version.

## Seeing it in game

An overlay's page has the command that opens it and an **Open in-game** button
that runs it for you:

```
/rp screen studio:<id> @p
```

That's [RP Engine](/rp-engine)'s command. It needs the `rpengine.screen`
permission (op by default); the button works without it. The screen is real,
so you see your art against real slots and items.

The Player Inventory and Escape Menu need no command: [Sync](/testing/plugin)
the pack and press <kbd>E</kbd> or <kbd>Esc</kbd>.

### Opening it from your own plugin

Without RP Engine, your plugin opens the inventory with the GUI's title, shown
on its page, in white:

```java
// Copy the title from the GUI's page in Studio.
Inventory inv = Bukkit.createInventory(null, 54,
    Component.text("").color(NamedTextColor.WHITE));
player.openInventory(inv);
```

The picture is drawn in the title's colour, so a coloured title tints the whole
screen.

<Callout type="note" title="Moving a GUI to another pack changes its title">
  The characters are assigned per pack, so a GUI copied into another pack gets
  new ones. Copy the title from its page again. A Player Inventory or Escape
  Menu won't copy into a pack that already has one.
</Callout>

## Exporting

An overlay leaves as a PNG under `assets/minecraft/textures/font/` plus an
entry in the pack's font file. Nothing under `textures/gui/` changes, which is
why ordinary screens stay ordinary.

The Player Inventory leaves as
`assets/minecraft/textures/gui/container/inventory.png`, and the Escape Menu as
vanilla's button sprites plus label overrides. Both work as soon as the pack is
applied.

## On Bedrock

GUIs are Java-only. Bedrock draws its screens differently. The rest of your
pack converts as normal.
