# Rank tags

A rank tag is the badge in front of a player's name: a rounded red **OWNER**,
a gold **VIP** with a crown, a blue **MOD** with a shine sweeping across it.
It's made to be a LuckPerms prefix or suffix, or the prefix of any other
permissions plugin.

![Animated rank tags made in Studio](/images/rank-tags.gif)

In the pack, a rank tag is an [icon](/icons): one character that draws the
picture anywhere text is drawn. The difference is that you never paint it.
It's built from parts (a shape, a face, a frame, the words, an icon, an
animation), so you change it by changing those, and it stays sharp whatever
you change.

## Making one

Open a pack, press **Add asset**, pick **Icon** and set **Type** to
**Rank tag**.

- **Generate**: name the rank and a colour. It takes a few seconds and opens in
  the editor. A single rank comes back as **three takes** to choose between; name
  several ranks (`VIP, MVP and MVP+`) and you get a **matching set**, one tag per
  rank, all for the price of one.
- **Blank**: type what it says, pick a colour and a look, and it opens in the
  editor.

A colour in the prompt is the **background**, unless you say it belongs to the
letters or the outline:

| Prompt | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| `a red OWNER tag` | A red tag, white letters |
| `an ADMIN tag with red text` | Red letters on a dark tag |
| `a gold VIP+ tag with a white plus` | A gold tag, the `+` in white |
| `a glossy BUILDER tag with a hammer` | A shiny tag with a hammer in a cell |
| `an OWNER tag with a shine` | A tag with a shine sweeping across it |
| `VIP, MVP and MVP+ tags` | A set of three that belong together |

With no colour, you get the one servers usually give that rank: red for
owner, blue for moderator, gold for VIP and so on.

## The editor

The tag sits large in the middle, drawn the way the game draws it (text
shadow included) and playing if it's animated. Under it, **Where it shows**
draws it at game size in the four places a server puts a rank: **Chat**, the
**Tab list**, the **Scoreboard** and **Above head**, each in the game's own font.
An animated tag plays in the first three; over a head the game shows its still
frame, so that one does too. Switch them between **Prefix** and **Suffix**, or
change the player name (your own, if you've linked your Minecraft account).

The panel on the right holds the tag's parts, one section each. A section
folds away with a click on its heading, and folded it still says what's in it
(**Frame · Auto outline · 3D edge**), so you can keep open only what you're
working on.

| Section | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Text** | The words, the **Font**, the **Colour** of the letters, the **Effect** on them (Shadow, Drop, Outline, Outline + shadow or None) with its colour, and a colour for the `+` part (VIP+, MVP++) |
| **Background** | The **Shape** (Square, Rounded, Pill, Chamfer, Ribbon, Slant or Arrow) and the **Surface** (Flat, Gradient, Gloss, Panel, Stripes, Dither, Sparkle, or **Block**: a real block texture, tinted toward the tag's colour so the words stay readable), each surface drawn on your own colours, and the **Fill** colour. A Block surface offers iron, gold, diamond and emerald a click away, and the **+** beside them opens every block in the game |
| **Frame** | The **Outline** (worked out from the background, a colour of your own, or none), an **Inner rim**, a **3D edge** that stands the tag up off the line, and the **Padding** beside the words |
| **Icon** | One of over a hundred icons, searchable, **In a cell** inside the tag, **Beside** the words or on a **Tile** of its own, and the cell's colour |
| **Motion** | Still, **Glint** (a shine sweeps across), **Rainbow** (the letters cycle colours), **Twinkle** (sparkles come and go) or **Pulse** (the frame breathes). Each plays on your own tag while you point at it, so you can see it before choosing |
| **In game** | **Size**, on a slider from a quarter size to 4×, with ¼×, ½×, 1×, 2× and 4× a click away, and **Height** on the line if it should sit higher or lower than centred, one pixel per step |

Every colour is one swatch: click it for the picker, with the colours already
in your tag, a palette of rank colours, a colour wheel and a hex code. A **+**
beside the letter colour fades the words to a second colour at the bottom, and
one beside the fill (or the **Gradient** surface) makes a gradient of up to three. Things a tag doesn't have
yet, like a rim, show as a **+** line; once added they get a cross to take them
away again. The small **i** beside a field says what it does.

**Looks**, at the bottom of the panel, restyles the whole tag in one click,
each drawn on your own tag: Classic, Rounded, Pill, Button, Panel, Frame,
Chamfer, Ribbon, Slant, Stripes, Gold block and Obsidian. The words, colour and
icon stay.

Colours you don't set work themselves out from the background: the outline is
a darker shade of it, and so are the shadow and the icon's cell. Set one
yourself and it stays.

**Fonts.** Block is chunky 5-pixel capitals, the classic rank look, and keeps a
tag exactly one line of chat tall. Minecraft is the game's own font, in any
case (Sr.Mod, Builder), and Bold is that font in bold. Your pack's own TrueType
fonts are there too: pick **Own** and **Upload a font**.

**Edit with AI**, in the top bar, changes the tag from a sentence: "make it
gold", "add a crown", "glossy with a 3D edge", "make it shine", "half the
size", "use my pack font". It can set everything the panel can. The change
lands in the fields as an unsaved edit, so you can keep it with **Save** or
undo it. It costs 5 AI Credits. When the AI made more than one take, they are there too: click one to use it.

**Save** keeps the tag's character, so a prefix that already uses it shows the
new look at once. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> undoes and
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> saves.

<Callout type="tip" title="One line of chat">
  A Block tag with an outline is 9 pixels tall, exactly one line of chat, so it
  never overlaps the lines around it. A letter outline, a 3D edge, the
  Minecraft font and bigger sizes make it taller. Save those for the scoreboard,
  the tab list and holograms.
</Callout>

**Sizes.** The whole sizes (1×, 2×, 3×, 4×) are pixel for pixel. A tag
smaller than 1× is smaller than chat's own letters: a ½× tag keeps every pixel
at GUI scale 2 and up, a ¼× tag only at GUI scale 4, and its words may not
read. A small tag can also only move so far up the line, because the game won't
draw a glyph higher than its own height. Between whole sizes the game scales the picture, as it does
any icon, so a few pixels come out a little wider than others; the line of chat
under the editor's preview shows it the way the game will.

To draw something on a tag by hand, use **Paint by hand**, beside Looks. It turns the tag
into a plain icon with the same picture and the same character, which the
pixel editor can open. Its parts stop being fields after that, an animated tag
keeps only its first frame, and a tag between whole sizes becomes the next
whole size up.

## Animated tags

An animated tag plays in game: in chat, the tab list, the scoreboard, anywhere
a menu or the HUD draws text. It loops sixteen frames at ten a second, exactly
as the editor plays it.

The pack's text shader plays it, so it needs your pack (the shader comes
inside it) and the colour its command gives it. Over a player's head, in a
nametag, it shows its first frame.

## Putting it on a rank

The tag's page writes the LuckPerms command for you. Type the group, pick
**Prefix** or **Suffix**, copy, and run it in the console or in chat:

```
/lp group owner meta setprefix 100 "&f<tag> "
```

`<tag>` stands for the tag's own character, which the page fills in for you:
it's a Private Use character, and nobody can type one by hand.

- **Keep the `&f`.** The game tints a glyph with the colour of the text it's in,
  so a tag after `&c` comes out tinted red. White leaves its colours alone.
- **An animated tag gets a colour of its own** in place of `&f`, written as a
  hex code like `&#fcfcf4`. That colour is what plays the animation, so keep it,
  and if your chat plugin writes hex colours another way, use the same six
  digits in its spelling (`<#fcfcf4>`, `{#fcfcf4}`). Keep the `&r` after the
  tag as well: without it many chat plugins draw the player's name in that
  colour, and the name shimmers along with the tag.
- **Priority** decides which prefix wins when a player is in several groups.
  Give higher ranks higher numbers.
- Players need your pack to see the tag. Without it, the character shows as a
  blank box.

Any chat, tab-list or nametag plugin that shows LuckPerms prefixes shows the
tag, because it's just a character in the prefix.

<Callout type="note">
  Chat draws a soft dark copy of every character one pixel down and to the
  right, and a tag gets one too. It's the game's ordinary text shadow, not part
  of your tag.
</Callout>

## Sharing it

**Publish to the Gallery** on the tag's page puts it in the
[Gallery](https://resourcepack.ai/gallery) with a page of its own: the tag
playing, and how it looks in chat, the tab list, the scoreboard and over a
head. Anybody can add it to a pack of their own, where it's drawn again from
its parts and edits like any other tag. A tag set in one of your pack's own
fonts takes the font with it.

## In a content folder

[RP Engine](/rp-engine) draws rank tags from a few lines of YAML in its
content folder, with the same parts and the same result, pixel for pixel. See
[RP Engine rank tags](/rp-engine/rank-tags).

## Exporting

A rank tag exports exactly as an icon does: its PNG under
`assets/minecraft/textures/font/` and its entry in
`assets/minecraft/font/default.json`. An animated tag's PNG is its first frame,
and the pack's text shader, which plays it, goes in the same zip. See
[Icons](/icons#exporting).
