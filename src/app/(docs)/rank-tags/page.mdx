# Rank Tags

A Rank Tag is the badge in front of a player's name: a rounded red **OWNER**,
a gold **VIP** with a crown, a blue **MOD** with a shine sweeping across it.
You make it in Studio and use it as a LuckPerms prefix or suffix (or the prefix
of any other permissions plugin).

![Animated rank tags made in Studio](/images/rank-tags.gif)

In your resource pack a Rank Tag is an [Icon](/icons): a single text
character that draws as the picture. Unlike an Icon you never paint it. It's
built from parts (a shape, a surface, a frame, the words, an icon, an
animation) and you change it by changing those.

## Making one

Open a pack, press **Add asset**, pick **Icon** and set **Type** to
**Rank Tag**. Then either:

- **Generate**: name the rank and a colour. It costs 5 credits and takes a few
  seconds. One rank comes back as **three takes** to choose from. Name several
  ranks (`VIP, MVP and MVP+`) and you get a **matching set**, one tag each, for
  the same price.
- **Blank**: type the words, pick a colour and a look.

Either way it opens in the editor. A colour in the prompt is the
**background**, unless you say it belongs to the letters or the outline:

| Prompt | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| `a red OWNER tag` | A red tag, white letters |
| `an ADMIN tag with red text` | Red letters on a dark tag |
| `a gold VIP+ tag with a white plus` | A gold tag, the `+` in white |
| `a glossy BUILDER tag with a hammer` | A shiny tag with a hammer in a cell |
| `an OWNER tag with a shine` | A tag with a shine sweeping across it |
| `VIP, MVP and MVP+ tags` | A set of three that belong together |

With no colour you get the one servers usually give that rank: red for owner,
blue for moderator, gold for VIP and so on.

## Putting it on a rank

1. On the tag's page, type the LuckPerms group, pick **Prefix** or **Suffix**,
   and copy the command it writes:

   ```
   /lp group owner meta setprefix 100 "&f<tag> "
   ```

   `<tag>` stands for the tag's own character, which the page fills in. You
   can't type it by hand, so always copy the command.
2. Run it in the console or in chat.
3. Make sure players have your pack: put it on your server with
   [Distribution](/distribution), or [export it](/exporting) and serve it
   yourself. Without the pack the tag shows as a blank box.

Any chat, tab-list or nametag plugin that shows LuckPerms prefixes then shows
the tag, because it's just a character in the prefix.

- **Keep the `&f`.** The game tints a character with the colour of the text
  it's in, so a tag after `&c` comes out red. White leaves its colours alone.
- **An animated tag uses a hex colour instead of `&f`**, like `&#fcfcf4`. That
  exact colour is what makes it play, so keep it. If your chat plugin writes hex
  another way, use the same six digits in its spelling (`<#fcfcf4>`,
  `{#fcfcf4}`). Keep the `&r` after the tag too, or many chat plugins draw the
  player's name in that colour and it shimmers along with the tag.
- **Priority** (the `100`) decides which prefix wins when a player is in
  several groups. Give higher ranks higher numbers.

The soft dark copy one pixel down and right of the tag in chat is the game's
ordinary text shadow, not part of your tag.

## The editor

The tag sits large in the middle, drawn the way the game draws it and playing
if it's animated. Under it, **Where it shows** draws it at game size in
**Chat**, the **Tab list**, the **Scoreboard** and **Above head**. Switch these
between prefix and suffix, or change the player name shown.

The panel on the right holds the tag's parts. Click a section heading to fold
it away.

| Section | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Text** | The words, the **Font**, the letter **Colour**, the **Effect** (Shadow, Drop, Outline, Outline + shadow or None) and its colour, and a separate colour for the `+` in VIP+ |
| **Background** | The **Shape** (Square, Rounded, Pill, Chamfer, Ribbon, Slant or Arrow), the **Surface** (Flat, Gradient, Gloss, Panel, Stripes, Dither, Sparkle, or **Block**: a real block texture tinted toward the tag's colour) and the **Fill** colour. Block offers iron, gold, diamond and emerald, and the **+** opens every block in the game |
| **Frame** | The **Outline** (automatic, your own colour, or none), an **Inner rim**, a **3D edge** that stands the tag up off the line, and the **Padding** round the words |
| **Icon** | Over a hundred built-in icons, or one of your pack's own [Icons](/icons) under **This pack**, placed **In a cell**, **Beside** the words or on a **Tile** of its own. If you redraw a pack icon later, the section offers **Use the new one** |
| **Motion** | None, **Glint** (a shine sweeps across), **Rainbow** (the letters cycle colours), **Twinkle** (sparkles come and go), **Pulse** (the frame breathes), or **Custom**: your own, on a timeline (see [Your own animation](#your-own-animation)). Point at one to preview it. **Animate with AI** under them makes a custom one from a sentence |
| **In game** | **Size**, from a quarter to 4x, and **Height**, to sit it higher or lower on the line |

Click any colour swatch for the picker. A **+** beside the letter colour fades
the words to a second colour; one beside the fill makes a gradient of up to
three. Colours you don't set follow the background (the outline, the shadow and
the icon's cell are darker shades of it) until you set them yourself.

**Looks**, at the bottom of the panel, restyles the whole tag in one click and
keeps the words, colour and icon: Classic, Rounded, Pill, Button, Panel, Frame,
Chamfer, Ribbon, Slant, Stripes, Gold block and Obsidian.

**Fonts.** **Block** is chunky 5-pixel capitals, the classic rank look, one
line of chat tall. **MC** is the game's own font in any case (Sr.Mod, Builder),
and **Bold** is that font in bold. **Own** uses a TrueType (`.ttf`) font you
upload to the pack.

Block and MC have English letters only. For a tag in Thai, Chinese, Japanese,
Russian or another alphabet, upload a font that has those letters under
**Own**. If the pack already has one, typing the words switches the tag to it.
See [Your own fonts](/dialogs#your-own-fonts) for file types and sizes.

**Edit with AI**, in the top bar, changes the tag from a sentence ("make it
gold", "add a crown", "make it shine", "half the size") for 5 credits. The
change arrives as an unsaved edit: keep it with **Save** or undo it.

**Save** keeps the tag's character, so a prefix already using it shows the new
look at once. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> undoes and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> +
<kbd>S</kbd> saves.

### Keeping it to one line of chat

A Block tag with an outline is 9 pixels tall, exactly one line of chat, so it
never overlaps the lines around it. A letter outline, a 3D edge, the MC font
and bigger sizes make it taller: save those for the scoreboard, the tab list
and holograms.

**Sizes.** 1x, 2x, 3x and 4x are pixel for pixel. Smaller than 1x is smaller
than chat's own letters: a ½x tag keeps every pixel at GUI scale 2 and up, a
¼x tag only at GUI scale 4. In-between sizes are scaled by the game, so a few
pixels come out a little wider than others. The chat line under the preview
shows it as the game will.

### Painting on it by hand

**Paint by hand**, beside Looks, turns the tag into a plain Icon with the same
picture and the same character, which the pixel editor can open. After that its
parts are no longer editable, an animated tag keeps only its first frame, and a
tag at an in-between size becomes the next whole size up.

## Animated tags

An animated tag plays in chat, the tab list, the scoreboard, and anywhere a
menu or the HUD draws text. A built-in animation loops sixteen frames at ten
a second, as the editor plays it. The animation is played by a text shader that comes inside
your pack, so players need the pack and the command's colour (see above). Over
a player's head it shows its first frame.

## Your own animation

Pick **Custom** under **Motion** and a timeline opens under the tag, like the
one in the model editor's Animate mode. It starts from the animation the tag
had, so a Glint becomes a shine you can change.

Each row is a **track**: one effect on one part of the tag.

| Effect | What it does | Its value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tint** | Washes the part toward a colour: white to flash, black to dim, red to burn | How far, 0 to 100% |
| **Hue** | Turns the colours round the colour wheel. **Spread** turns each column a little more, which makes a rainbow across. White, black and greys have no hue to turn, so tint white words first (Start from Rainbow does) | How far, 0 to 360° |
| **Shine** | A diagonal band of light with soft edges, as wide as you set | Where the band is, 0% (before the tag) to 100% (past it) |
| **Sparkle** | Stars, somewhere new every frame | How many |
| **Wave** | Bands of light running along the tag, as long and as fast as you set (a negative speed runs them backwards) | How bright, 0 to 100% |
| **Particles** | Small things that fall, rise, drift or twinkle: rain, snow, embers, hearts. See [Particles](#particles) | About how many are on the tag at once |
| **Paint** | Frames you paint yourself, pixel by pixel. See [Painting frames](#painting-frames) | How strongly the paint shows, 0 to 100% |

A track lights the **Whole tag**, **Behind the words** (the whole tag except its
letters), the **Face**, the **Words**, the **Frame** (outline and rim), the
**Icon cell** or the **Icon**. Tint, Hue, Shine, Sparkle and Wave never touch
the outline and shadow of the words, so the words stay readable whatever the
face is doing. Tracks run top to bottom, each on what the one above left.

The diamonds are **keyframes**. Each sits at a frame and at a height, which is
its value: drag one sideways to move it in time and up or down to change it, or
pick it and set its value exactly under the timeline. Double-click a row for a
new one, and <kbd>Delete</kbd> takes the picked one away. **To the next key**
says how a key travels to the next: **Linear**, **Smooth** (eases in and out) or
**Hold** (stays put, then jumps). The line through the diamonds is what the
game plays.

The keys loop: after the last one the value travels back to the first, so a
loop that starts and ends on the same value never jumps. For a shine that
crosses and starts again, put a key at 0% on the first frame and one at 100% on
the last.

Above the rows: **Frames** is one loop's length (4 to 48; changing it stretches
the keys to fit), and **5, 10 or 20 fps** is how fast it plays. <kbd>Space</kbd>
plays and pauses, the arrow keys step a frame, and clicking the ruler jumps
there. **+ Track** adds one (up to six), the eye mutes one without losing it,
and **Start from** replaces the timeline with one of the built-in animations to
build on.

### Particles

A Particles track puts small moving things on the tag. Pick the track and its
settings open under the timeline. **Start from** fills them all in for Rain,
Snow, Embers, Fire, Bubbles, Stars, Confetti, Hearts, Code rain or a Shooting
star, and every one of them is then yours to change:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Colours** | Up to three. **Solid** is how much of a colour is laid on: lower it for glassy rain. With more than one colour, each particle is one of them, or goes through them all as it ages (a flame: pale yellow, then orange, then red) |
| **Shape** | Dot, Glow, Plus, Square, Dash, Ring, Flake or Heart, or **Your own**: a five by five grid where a click makes a pixel solid, a second click soft, a third clears it |
| **Across** and **Down** | How far it moves each frame. Down below zero rises. Both at none, it stays put and twinkles |
| **Trail** | Pixels fading behind it: two or three for rain, five for a shooting star |
| **Lasts** | How many frames each one lives. At zero it crosses the whole tag. A short life with a rising particle makes flames that die out part way up |
| **Fades** | In, out, both or not at all |
| **Wanders** | How far it drifts side to side as it goes: snow, embers, bubbles |
| **Speeds differ** | At zero they all move alike, which looks mechanical; 40% looks natural |
| **Appears** | **At the edge** it comes in from, or **Anywhere** on the tag |
| **Shuffle** | Another arrangement of the same particles |

Its keyframes are how many there are, so a storm can build and ease. Particles
on **Behind the words** pass over the face, the frame and the letters' outline
and never over the letters, which is what keeps a tag readable in the rain.

### Painting frames

A Paint track is drawn by hand. Pick it and the tag above the timeline takes
the brush: drag on it to paint the frame the playhead is on, and use the right
mouse button (or the **Eraser**) to take paint off. The arrow keys step a frame
at a time, and a mark under the track shows which frames have paint.

Its **Palette** holds up to fifteen colours, each with its own **Solid**. Under
**Frame**, **Last frame** copies the paint of the frame before, **Every frame**
puts this frame's paint on all of them, **Clear** takes it off, and the arrows
under **Move it** nudge it a pixel at a time, so a moving thing is a copy and a
nudge per frame. Its keyframes are how strongly the paint shows, which lets
painted frames fade in and out.

Paint lies only on pixels the tag has (it cannot go outside the tag's shape)
and only on the part the track is **On**.

### How much a tag can hold

Painted frames and particles are stored inside the tag's own picture, and a tag
only has so much room for them: the smaller and narrower the tag, the more.
**Paint** beside **Start from** shows how much is taken. Past 100% the tag
won't save. To get room back, paint fewer frames or use fewer colours, turn the
particles down or shorten their trails, make the loop shorter, or make the tag
narrower or smaller under **In game**.

### Animate with AI

**Animate with AI**, on the timeline or under **Motion**, writes the timeline
from a sentence: "rain falling behind the words", "embers rising off the
bottom", "a slow gold shimmer", "flash white every second", "rainbow letters
with sparkles on the face", "the frame burns like lava". Name a thing and it
puts that thing on the tag, with particles or painted frames where it needs
them. It looks at your tag first, then plays what it wrote frame by frame and
fixes what it sees before you get it: rain you cannot see, a shine too faint to
notice, words that get hard to read, a loop that jumps where it starts again.

A new animation comes in three versions, playing side by side under
**Versions**. The first lands on the tracks, and clicking another swaps it in.
Once your tag plays an animation of its own, you choose between **Change this
one** (one version, changed as asked) and **New ideas** (three new versions).
New ideas cost more than a change; the price is beside the button. Whatever
lands is an unsaved edit, like Edit with AI: **Save** keeps it and undo takes
it back. The versions stay in the popover until you leave the editor.

A custom animation plays in game the same way the built-in ones do, in its own
colour, which the tag's page puts in the command. A pack can hold sixteen tags
with their own animation.

If you run your pack through an image optimiser, tell it to leave font
textures exactly as they are (no colour reduction, and no changes to
transparent pixels): an animated tag keeps what it needs in them. RP Engine's
PackSquash setup already does.

## Sharing it

**Publish to Gallery** on the tag's page puts it in the
[Gallery](https://resourcepack.ai/gallery). Anybody can add it to a pack of
their own and edit it like any other tag. A tag in one of your own fonts takes
the font with it.

## In a content folder

[RP Engine](/rp-engine) can draw the same tags from a few lines of YAML in its
content folder (the folder of files on your server it builds a pack from). See
[RP Engine rank tags](/rp-engine/rank-tags).

## Exporting

A Rank Tag exports as an Icon does: its PNG under
`assets/minecraft/textures/font/` and its entry in
`assets/minecraft/font/default.json`. An animated tag's PNG is its first frame,
and the text shader that plays it goes in the same zip. See
[Icons](/icons#exporting).
