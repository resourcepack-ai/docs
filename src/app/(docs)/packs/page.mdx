# Packs

A pack is one project: everything for one server or one look. It becomes one
resource pack in Minecraft. Each thing in it (one Block texture, one 3D Model,
one Sound) is an **asset**. You test, share and export a whole pack at once.

The pack page has a tab for each kind of asset: Models, Vehicles, Mobs, Blocks,
Items, Armor, GUIs, HUD, Dialogs, Icons, Sounds, Emotes and Shaders. 2D and 3D
Items share Items, 2D and 3D Armor share Armor, Rank Tags sit under Icons, and
Text Effects and VFX under Shaders.

## Making a pack

Press **New pack** on the dashboard. Studio suggests a name and an icon; use
the dice to pick others, or upload your own image. Both can be changed any
time.

Already have a resource pack as a `.zip`? Press [Import](/importing) beside
it instead, which makes a new pack from the file.

## Copying, renaming and deleting

Right-click a pack card (or open its menu) for **Open pack**, **Edit pack**,
**Duplicate**, **Export**, **Change icon** and **Delete pack**. Duplicate
copies the whole pack, assets and all, so you can experiment without risking
the original.

**Edit pack** changes the name and description. It also has one setting that
matters once you've invited other people: whose AI Credits they spend when
they generate. See [Collaborators](/collaborators#whose-credits-get-spent).

## Sorting a pack into folders

Folders keep a big pack tidy. They're listed beside the grid like folders in
a file manager (on a narrower screen, as a row above the tabs). Click one and
the grid shows what's in it; click the pack's name above them to see
everything again.

Each folder holds one kind of asset: a folder of models, a folder of icons, a
folder of emotes. The **+** beside the list's title makes one, asking for a
name, what it holds and a colour. Click the title itself to choose what the
list shows: every folder **A–Z**, or only the folders of one kind. The button
beside the **+** tucks the list away to a small **Folders** tab, and it stays
tucked away until you open it again.

**Show folders**, with the pack's buttons at the top, decides how the grid
treats them. On (the default), folders also appear as tiles at the top of the pack, and
whatever is filed in one is tucked inside it rather than shown in the grid;
hover a tile to see the first few things inside. Off, every asset stays in the
grid where it always was, with a small folder mark on anything filed, and the
folder list is how you narrow it down. The setting is remembered.

To put things in a folder:

- **Drag** a card onto it, in the folder list or onto its tile. Drag a card
  you've ticked and the whole selection comes with it.
- **Tick several** and use **Move to folder** in the bar that comes up. The
  same menu can make a new folder for them on the spot.
- **Right-click** a card and choose **Move to folder…**

To take something out, drag it onto the pack's name in the folder list, or use
**Move to folder → Take out of folder**. Searching from the top of the pack
looks inside every folder, and each result shows which folder it's in.

Folders only change how the pack page is laid out, never what the pack exports
or what reaches a server. Deleting a folder puts everything in it back at the
top of the pack rather than deleting it. Folders
are one level deep, and an asset is in at most one.

## Saving

Everything saves as you go; there's no save button and no build step. **Sync**
(which sends the pack to your game, see the [Quickstart](/quickstart)) and
[Export](/exporting) always use what's in the pack right now.

## Which Minecraft versions it works on

You don't set this. Studio works it out from what's in the pack, and the pack
card shows the oldest version that will load it, for example "1.21.4+".

A pack with nothing special in it works on **1.19.4** and newer. Some assets
need a newer game:

| Asset | Needs |
| --- | --- |
| A reskin of a block or item added in a later update (pale oak, say) | The update that added it |
| An Item variant (a second look for an item, handed out by command) | 1.21.4+ |
| A 3D Model with glowing parts (light emission) | 1.21.3+ |
| A 3D Model with its own position for shelves | 1.21.9+ |
| A Dialog | 1.21.6+ |

Each asset shows its own minimum as a chip on its page, with the reason; for a
model, it names the cubes responsible. Delete or change that asset and the
pack works on older versions again.
