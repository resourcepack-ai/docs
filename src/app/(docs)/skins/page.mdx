# Skins

A skin is what a player looks like. Skins live on your account rather than in
a pack, under **Skins** in the sidebar, because a skin is yours wherever you
play.

![A chef skin made in Studio, from the front, walking and from the back](/images/skins.webp)

## Making one

Press **New Skin** and choose how to start:

- **Generate**: describe it ("a knight in dented steel armour with a red
  cloak") and Studio paints it, starting from the default skin so the face and
  shape are right. It costs AI Credits (from 45) and takes a few minutes; the
  skin shows as generating until it lands.
- **Import**: drop a skin PNG you already have, or type a Minecraft username
  to copy that player's skin.
- **Start from**: open the editor on the default skin or an empty body and
  paint it yourself.

Every skin has an **arm width**, **Classic** or **Slim**, which you can change
later.

## The editor

You paint the skin flat, beside a 3D player you can turn to check it.
The **outer layer** sits just off the body, which is how hair, hoods, sleeves
and jackets stand proud of it; switch to it to paint there. The tools are the
pixel editor's: pencil, fill, a colour picker, rectangles and ellipses, and
**Fill part** to colour a whole body part at once.

## Using it

- **Sync**: puts the skin on you on the server your pack is linked to. It
  changes how you look to everybody there.
- **Download PNG**: the 64×64 file Minecraft takes, to set as your skin in the
  launcher.
- **Publish to the Gallery**: anybody can then copy it.

Syncing goes through a service that registers the skin with Minecraft, which
is what makes other players see it. It is rate limited, so on a busy day a
sync can be refused for a minute; try again shortly.
