# Mobs

A mob is a model that walks around and fights: a golem boss, a raccoon, a
beetle. You make the model as usual, convert it, and then choose how it
behaves, how tough it is, which animation plays when, what it sounds like and
what it drops. Converting changes nothing about the model itself. The cubes,
textures and animations are still edited in the model editor.

Mobs are run by [RP Engine](/testing/plugin) 0.2.51 or newer, so they need it
on the server. Our test server has it.

## Making one

1. **Make the model.** Generate, upload or build it, as on the
   [Models](/models) page. Give it bones and animations (walk, idle, attack)
   in the model editor's Animate mode, because a mob plays them by state.
2. **Convert it.** On the model's page, press **Convert** in the header, or
   right-click the model's card and choose **Convert to…**, then **Mob**. The
   mob editor opens straight away. From then on it's on the pack's **Mobs**
   tab rather than under Models.
3. **Set it up.** A new mob already works: a hostile mob with 20 health. Change
   what you need and it saves on its own.

## The mob editor

The model stands in the middle with its hitbox drawn round it and a blue arrow
at its feet showing which way it walks. The states and sounds are on the left,
everything else on the right.

### Behaviour

Underneath, a mob is a vanilla mob you can't see, wearing your model. Its
behaviour decides which one, and so how it moves and fights.

| Behaviour | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Hostile** | Chases and hits players |
| **Brute** | Big, slow, hits hard |
| **Climber** | Hostile, and climbs walls |
| **Neutral** | Wanders, and fights back when hit |
| **Guardian** | Fights hostile mobs |
| **Passive** | Wanders, and runs when hit |
| **Flying** | Drifts around in the air |
| **Flying hostile** | Swoops at players |
| **Statue** | Never moves |

**Burns in daylight** is off unless you tick it, whatever the mob walks like.

### Stats

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Health** | In half-hearts: 20 is ten hearts, the same as a player |
| **Damage** | Per hit, in half-hearts. 0 keeps the vanilla mob's own |
| **Speed** | A multiple of the vanilla mob's own speed |
| **Knockback resistance** | How much of a hit's knockback it ignores |
| **XP** | Experience it drops when killed |

### Size and facing

**Model size** is how big the model is drawn. **Hitbox height** is how tall
the box it's hit and pushed by is, in blocks; its width grows with it. Press
**Fit to model** to match it to the model at its size. The hitbox needs a
1.20.5 or newer server; older ones keep the vanilla mob's box.

If the mob walks sideways or backwards, use **Turn** until the blue arrow
points out of its face. **Head** picks a bone that turns to look at what the
mob is after. That bone has to move in at least one animation, or it's drawn
as part of the body and can't turn.

### Animations

One of the model's animations per state. Press play beside a state to watch it
in the editor.

| State | When it plays |
| --- | --- |
| **Death** | Once, when it dies. The body stays until it ends (at most 5 seconds) |
| **Spawn** | Once, as it appears |
| **Attack** | Once, each time it hits something |
| **Hurt** | Once, each time it takes damage |
| **Run** | Moving fast or chasing. Blank plays Walk |
| **Walk** | Moving. Blank plays Idle |
| **Idle** | Standing still. Blank leaves it in its rest pose |

The list is in order of importance: a mob doing several things at once plays
the first one listed. The first four play once to the end unless something
higher up starts; a blank one simply doesn't play.

### Sounds

Ambient, hurt, death, attack and step, each a sound from the pack. Pick one,
or press **+** to make a new one in the sound editor (it opens in a new tab,
and the list updates when you come back). Setting any of them silences the
vanilla mob underneath, so a mob with none set keeps its own sounds. Ambient
plays every so often while a player is nearby.

### Drops

What it drops when killed, instead of the vanilla mob's loot: an item, the
least and most of it, and the chance it drops at all. Search for any vanilla
item, or type `studio:<id>` for one of the pack's own items. With no drops it
drops nothing but its XP.

## Spawning it in game

1. **Sync the pack** to a server running RP Engine.
2. Press **Spawn in game** on the model's page (or **Give** on its card) to get
   a spawn egg for it. Use the egg on a block to hatch the mob there. This needs
   a 1.20.5 or newer pack.
3. Or use the commands on the server:

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `/rp spawn studio:<id>` | Spawns one where you stand |
| `/rp egg studio:<id> [player] [amount]` | Gives a spawn egg for it |

`<id>` is the model's id, shown on its page.

## Exports

A resource pack has no way to say a model is a mob, so the mob settings travel
alongside a synced pack and RP Engine reads them there. They're also written
into an exported Java pack's `studio-content.json`, which RP Engine reads when
the zip is dropped into its content folder. The other exports carry the model,
but not the mob.
