# Mobs

A mob is a model that walks around and fights: a golem boss, a raccoon, a
beetle. You make the model as usual, convert it, and then choose how it
behaves, how tough it is, which animation plays when, what it sounds like and
what it drops. Converting changes nothing about the model itself. The cubes,
textures and animations are still edited in the model editor.

Mobs are run by [RP Engine](/testing/plugin) 0.2.51 or newer, so they need it
on the server. Our test server has it.

## Generating one from words

The quickest way to a mob is to describe it.

1. On the pack page, press **Add asset**, stay on the **Model** tab, and set
   **What is it** to **Mob**.
2. Describe the creature: *"a waffle and cake golem boss with a crocodile
   jaw"*, *"a raccoon"*, *"a purple cave spider"*. A reference picture works
   here as it does for any model.
3. Press **Generate**. The model is built as a creature made to be animated
   (its head, jaw, legs, arms, tail and wings are separate parts), then it's
   set up and animated in the same run: idle, walk, attack, hurt and death.
   Last, it gets **sounds of its own**: an idle call, a hurt yelp, a death cry
   and an attack, designed from what the creature is (a raccoon chitters, a
   golem rumbles). Its card says it's generating, then animating.

The price shown under the prompt covers all of it, and it's held while it runs
and settled to what the run actually used. If the model is built but the
animating fails, you keep the mob without animations and aren't charged for
the animating. A sound that can't be made is skipped the same way: the mob
keeps the vanilla sound for it and you aren't charged for it. The mob editor
says what went wrong, and **Animate with AI** or **Generate sounds with AI**
tries again.

## Making one by hand

1. **Make the model.** Generate, upload or build it, as on the
   [Models](/models) page. Give it bones and animations (walk, idle, attack)
   in the model editor's Animate mode, because a mob plays them by state.
2. **Convert it.** On the model's page, press **Convert** in the header, or
   right-click the model's card and choose **Convert to…**, then **Mob**. The
   mob editor opens straight away. From then on it's on the pack's **Mobs**
   tab rather than under Models.
3. **Set it up.** A new mob already works: a hostile mob with 20 health. Change
   what you need and it saves on its own.

## Animate with AI

At the foot of the mob editor's left column. It gives every part that moves a
bone with the right pivot, then writes one animation for each state you tick,
all at once, and points each state at its animation.

- **What is it, and how does it move?** Optional words that steer it: *"a
  heavy golem that stomps and bites"*, *"hops instead of walking"*.
- **Set it up too** also chooses the behaviour, health, damage, speed and
  experience from the description, measures the hitbox off the model, works out
  which way it faces and picks its head. It's on for a mob that has no
  animations yet. Off, your settings are left alone and it only animates.
  For a mob with no abilities yet, it also gives it the ones your words ask
  for: *"a boss that slams the ground and summons minions at half health"*
  comes back with a Ground Slam and a phase that calls minions, and with a
  `slam` and a `summon` animation written for them beside the states (up to
  two such animations, inside the same price). A VFX or sound an ability
  wants that the pack doesn't have is left as a button on that step, with its
  price, for you to make or not; nothing extra is generated on its own.
- **States to animate**: Idle, Walk, Attack, Hurt and Death are ticked to start
  with. Run and Spawn are there if you want them; a blank Run plays Walk.

Walk and the other loops repeat without a jump, death ends with the mob lying
still, and the one-shots return to the rest pose so the next state starts
cleanly. Running it again replaces the animations it writes and keeps any other
animation the model has. It runs in the background: leave the editor and come
back, and the states fill in when it finishes.

## The mob editor

The model stands in the middle with its hitbox drawn round it and a blue arrow
at its feet showing which way it walks. The states and sounds are on the left,
everything else on the right.

### Behaviour

Underneath, a mob is a vanilla mob you can't see, wearing your model. Its
behaviour decides which one, and so how it moves and fights.

| Behaviour | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Hostile** | Chases and hits players |
| **Brute** | Big, slow, hits hard |
| **Climber** | Hostile, and climbs walls |
| **Ranged** | Keeps its distance and shoots |
| **Neutral** | Wanders, and fights back when hit |
| **Guardian** | Fights hostile mobs |
| **Passive** | Wanders, and runs when hit |
| **Flying** | Drifts around in the air |
| **Flying hostile** | Swoops at players |
| **Statue** | Never moves |

**Burns in daylight** is off unless you tick it, whatever the mob walks like.
**Flashes red when hurt** is on: the model goes red for a moment when it takes
damage, like a vanilla mob. On servers older than 1.21.4 it shows a red outline
instead. **Fire immune** stops fire and lava hurting it, and **Glowing outline**
gives it the outline a spectral arrow does.

A **Ranged** mob gets a **Shooting** section: what it fires (an arrow, a
snowball, a small fireball, a fireball, a wither skull or a trident), the damage
per hit (0 keeps the projectile's own), and how far away it shoots from. It
keeps its distance like a skeleton and plays its Attack animation each time it
shoots. A fireball or a skull never breaks blocks.

### Stats

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Health** | In half-hearts: 20 is ten hearts, the same as a player |
| **Damage** | Per hit, in half-hearts. 0 keeps the vanilla mob's own |
| **Speed** | A multiple of the vanilla mob's own speed |
| **Knockback resistance** | How much of a hit's knockback it ignores |
| **XP** | Experience it drops when killed |
| **Notices targets from** | How far away, in blocks, it spots something to chase. Blank keeps the vanilla mob's own |

### Boss bar

Turn on **Show a boss bar** for a bar across the top of the screen with the
mob's name and health, shown to every player within **Seen within** blocks.
Pick its colour and how many notches it has.

### Spawning

Turn on **Spawns on its own** and the mob appears in the world by itself. It
**takes the place of** some of the mobs the game spawns naturally rather than
adding to them, so your world never holds more mobs than it would have:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Share of natural spawns** | How many of the matching natural spawns become this mob |
| **Replaces** | The vanilla mobs it stands in for. None picked is any natural spawn of its own kind: monsters for a Hostile, Brute, Climber, Ranged or Flying hostile mob, animals for a Neutral or Passive one. Guardian and Flying mobs need at least one picked |
| **Biomes** | Where it spawns. None picked is every biome. Type a name with `*` in it, like `*forest*`, to match several |
| **When** | Any time, only by day, or only at night |
| **Light** | The light level where it appears, 0 to 15 |
| **Group** | How many appear together |
| **Per chunk** | Spawning stops adding more once a chunk holds this many |
| **Worlds** | The world names it spawns in. None picked is every world |

A mob that spawned on its own despawns when nobody is near, the way the mob it
replaced would have. One you spawn with a command or an egg stays.

### Size and facing

**Model size** is how big the model is drawn. **Hitbox height** is how tall
the box it's hit and pushed by is, in blocks; its width grows with it. Press
**Fit to model** to match it to the model at its size. The hitbox needs a
1.20.5 or newer server; older ones keep the vanilla mob's box.

If the mob walks sideways or backwards, use **Turn** until the blue arrow
points out of its face. **Head** picks a bone that turns to look at what the
mob is after. That bone has to move in at least one animation, or it's drawn
as part of the body and can't turn.

### Animations

One of the model's animations per state. Press play beside a state to watch it
in the editor.

| State | When it plays |
| --- | --- |
| **Death** | Once, when it dies. The body stays until it ends (at most 5 seconds) |
| **Spawn** | Once, as it appears |
| **Attack** | Once, each time it hits something |
| **Hurt** | Once, each time it takes damage |
| **Run** | Moving fast or chasing. Blank plays Walk |
| **Walk** | Moving. Blank plays Idle |
| **Idle** | Standing still. Blank leaves it in its rest pose |

The list is in order of importance: a mob doing several things at once plays
the first one listed. The first four play once to the end unless something
higher up starts; a blank one simply doesn't play.

### Sounds

Ambient, hurt, death, attack and step, each a sound from the pack. Pick one,
or press **+** to make a new one in the sound editor (it opens in a new tab,
and the list updates when you come back). The play button beside a slot plays
what it's set to. Setting any of them silences the vanilla mob underneath, so
a mob with none set keeps its own sounds. Ambient plays every so often while a
player is nearby.

**Generate sounds with AI**, under the slots, makes the ambient, hurt, death
and attack sounds for you, which is how a mob made before generated mobs had
sounds, or one converted from a model, gets its own. Describe the creature
(it starts from how the mob was asked for) and tick the sounds to make. Each
comes out short (about half a second for hurt and attack, one and a half to
two for ambient and death), mono so it comes from the mob in game, and
levelled so the four sit together. They're ordinary sounds of the pack, named
after the mob, with a second take on each sound's page if you prefer it. It's
charged like generating a sound, per take, and the price is shown before you
start.

### Abilities

What it does besides walk and bite: a ground slam every few seconds, minions
when it drops below half health, a poison aura, a volley of fireballs, a
phase where it goes red and fast. Each ability is three things:

- **When**: every few seconds, when it's hurt, when it hits something, when it
  appears, when it finds a target, when it dies, when a player right-clicks
  it or when it kills a player. Narrow it with **Health** (*Once below* 50%
  runs the moment it first drops under half, which is how a boss changes
  phase; *While below* runs every time while it's under) and conditions:
  only while it has a target, while its target is within or further than so
  many blocks, while a player is near, in or out of a phase, by day or night,
  in the rain, on the ground.
- **Do**: steps, run in order. Play one of its animations, wait, damage,
  heal, knock back, pull in, leap or lunge at its target, a potion effect,
  set on fire, summon mobs, enter a phase, play one of the pack's VFX or
  sounds, particles, a chat message or a title, shoot a projectile, an
  explosion (it never breaks blocks), lightning, tint the model, change speed,
  a shield, teleport to its target.
- **At whom**, per step: itself, its target, whoever set it off, players or
  everything within so many blocks, the nearest player, everything in front of
  it, or for effects a spot: where it stands, where its target stands, a spot
  ahead, a ring of spots, random spots near it.

Plus a cooldown and a chance. **Add an ability** starts from nothing or from
one of six: Ground slam, Summon minions at half health, Leap at its target,
Poison aura, Fireball volley and Enrage phase.

The **`</>`** button on an ability shows it as the
[MythicMobs](https://mythiccraft.io) skill lines RP Engine runs, and you can
edit it as lines: the first line says when, every line after it is a step.
Anything RP Engine supports works there, not only what the form offers, and a
line it can't read is pointed out as you type. Every trigger, target, condition
and step RP Engine runs is listed on [Skills](/rp-engine/skills). Press the button again to go
back to the form, which works as long as the lines only use what the form can
show.

Abilities need RP Engine 0.2.53 or newer. They reach the server with the rest
of the mob when you sync; a mob on an older RP Engine walks and fights as
before without them.

### Drops

What it drops when killed, instead of the vanilla mob's loot: an item, the
least and most of it, and the chance it drops at all. Search for any vanilla
item, or type `studio:<id>` for one of the pack's own items. With no drops it
drops nothing but its XP.

## Spawning it in game

1. **Sync the pack** to a server running RP Engine.
2. Press **Spawn in game** on the model's page (or **Give** on its card) to get
   a spawn egg for it. Use the egg on a block to hatch the mob there. This needs
   a 1.20.5 or newer pack.
3. Or use the commands on the server:

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `/rp spawn studio:<id>` | Spawns one where you stand |
| `/rp egg studio:<id> [player] [amount]` | Gives a spawn egg for it |

`<id>` is the model's id, shown on its page.

Bedrock players joining through Geyser see the mob as its model too, animated
the same way, from the Bedrock pack a sync sends them (RP Engine 0.2.53 or
newer). See [Bedrock and Geyser](/rp-engine/bedrock#custom-mobs-and-bound-models).

## Exports

A resource pack has no way to say a model is a mob, so the mob settings travel
alongside a synced pack and RP Engine reads them there. They're also written
into an exported Java pack's `studio-content.json`, which RP Engine reads when
the zip is dropped into its content folder. The other exports carry the model,
but not the mob.
