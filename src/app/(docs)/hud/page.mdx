# HUD

The HUD is everything Minecraft draws over the world when no screen is open:
hearts, the hunger row, armour, air bubbles, the hotbar, the experience bar,
the boss bar and the crosshair. A HUD asset gives all of it one theme from one
description, and you can finish it by hand afterwards.

![A HUD made in Studio: hotbar, hearts, hunger, armour, experience bar and crosshair](/images/hud.webp)

<Callout type="note" title="Reskin, not add">
  A HUD asset changes what vanilla's pieces look like. It can't add new ones,
  such as a thirst meter, an ammo counter or a quest tracker. For those, build
  a [shader object](/shaders): your own HUD element placed anywhere on screen,
  with live values like `{health}`.
</Callout>

A pack holds one HUD, because each piece replaces a vanilla file.

## Making one

Open a pack, press **Add asset** and pick **HUD**. Describe the theme, for
example "rusted iron and dried blood" or "frosty winter".

Under **Parts to redraw**, every part starts ticked. Untick the ones you want
left vanilla; the price follows what's ticked, because you're charged per
part. Naming a part in your description ("make the hunger bar look like
roasted meat") ticks it back on, and a line under the list says so. It matches
words like hearts, hunger or food, armor, air or bubbles, xp, crosshair,
hotbar and boss bar.

| Part | What one drawing becomes |
| --- | --- |
| **Hearts** | Every heart: full, half and empty, in the poisoned, withered, frozen, absorbing and hardcore moods, with and without the damage flash |
| **Hunger** | The food row, including the version drawn while you have Hunger |
| **Armor** | The armour row |
| **Air** | The breath bubbles |
| **Hotbar** | The tray, its selection box, the offhand slot and the attack indicator |
| **Experience bar** | The groove and the fill |
| **Boss bar** | All seven colours, groove and fill, plus the notched dividers |
| **Crosshair** | Picked from a list, not generated (free) |

Icons keep vanilla's shapes: your theme, palette and material, on the
silhouette players already recognise. So spend your words on the look ("rusted
iron"), not the object ("a drumstick made of iron").

A few details:

- **The hotbar stays see-through**, like vanilla's, with a solid outline.
- **The hardcore heart** is your heart with a harder outline, not vanilla's
  spiked drawing.
- **The boss bar** comes in seven colours that a plugin picks between, so one
  HUD gives a server several themed bars that don't look alike.

### Crosshair styles

A pack can't use vanilla's colour-inverting crosshair, so a replacement is
drawn as a light shape with a dark outline to stay visible everywhere. Pick
one: **Cross**, **Dot**, **Brackets**, **Circle** or **Chevron**. It's drawn in
your HUD's palette.

### Starting from vanilla

The **Blank** door makes a HUD from vanilla's own art for free, no prompt
needed. It opens straight in the HUD editor, which is the quick way to "vanilla,
but golden hearts".

## Screen overlays

The layers drawn over the whole screen (**Vignette**, **Nausea**, **Frost
rim**, **Spyglass scope**, **Pumpkin blur** and **Underwater tint**) are a
second HUD set. Studio's Add asset sheet doesn't offer it yet; it can be made
through the [public API](/api-reference) by asking for the `overlays` set. A
pack can have one HUD and one set of overlays.

## The HUD editor

Every sprite is laid out on one sheet at its real size, with a guide box round
each. The **Parts** rail jumps to a group, and the sprite under your cursor is
named on screen. The gaps between sprites aren't part of any file, so paint
that strays into them does nothing in game.

The **Preview** tab arranges your sheet into a live HUD over a day, cave or
Nether backdrop, and updates as you paint.

## Seeing it in game

[Sync](/testing/plugin) the pack and look at your screen. Take a hit to see the
damage flash, or go underwater for the air row. No command is needed.

## Exporting

A HUD leaves as the vanilla files it replaces, under
`assets/minecraft/textures/gui/sprites/hud/` and
`assets/minecraft/textures/gui/sprites/boss_bar/`, with `.png.mcmeta` files for
the few sprites that need one. No plugin is involved.

## On Bedrock

The HUD is Java-only. The rest of your pack converts as normal.
