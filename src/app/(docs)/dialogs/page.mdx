# Dialogs

A dialog is a screen a server opens on a player, added to Minecraft in 1.21.6.
Its buttons run commands, it can scroll, and it looks like part of the game.
Studio lets you design one the way you would design an app screen: sidebars,
cards, switches, sliders, item slots, pictures and pages, all in Minecraft's own
style.

![A settings dialog made in Studio, open in game](/images/dialogs.webp)

Use a dialog for a settings menu, a shop, a server guide, a warp list, a
profile card, a punishment panel, or anything else that is a screen with
choices on it. Unlike a chest [GUI](/guis), nothing can be dragged in or out
of it, though it can still show items with their tooltips, and even let a
player rearrange their own inventory.

## What you need

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Minecraft | **1.21.6 or newer** on the server and the client. Older versions have no dialog screen; use a [GUI](/guis) there. |
| A plugin | [RP Engine](/rp-engine) or [Dialog Engine](/dialog-engine) to open it, and to fill in everything that changes per player. A plain datapack also works, with less (see [Without RP Engine](#without-rp-engine)). |
| The resource pack | Everything drawn on the screen is pictures in your pack, so players need the pack too. |

Keep the plugin up to date. Each advanced feature on this page needs a recent
version, listed in [Plugin versions](#plugin-versions).

## Making one

Dialogs have their own **Dialogs** tab on the pack page. Make one under **Add
asset**:

- **Generate**: describe the screen and the AI designs it. See [Generating
  one](#generating-one).
- **Blank**: start from nothing (a title, a line of words and a **Close**
  button) or from a **template**: an event shop, a quest board, a server hub, a
  rank store, daily rewards, a crate, a profile, warps, rules or a welcome
  screen. Each comes with a banner, a kit and its pieces, all yours to change.

Then pair the pack with your server and push it, the same way you [test
anything else](/testing/plugin), and open it in game:

```
/rp dialogs                     # list the dialogs on the server
/rp dialog studio:quest-board   # open one
```

The dialog's settings panel in the editor shows its exact id.

### Generating one

Say what the screen is for: "a settings menu with video, audio and chat", "a
pet menu where you can rename and summon your pet", "a crate menu for a prison
server". The AI first decides how it should look, choosing a [kit](#kits),
colours (or the ones you name, or your pack's [Style
Guide](/generating#writing-a-prompt) palette), a banner and what should move.
Then it builds the screen from the editor's own pieces, draws the banner's
picture, and looks at the result as a player would. It fixes cut-off labels and
screens that are too tall, and makes one thing the focal point, before saving.

- A dialog costs **30 AI Credits**.
- **Variations** makes up to three dialogs from one prompt, each with a
  different look, at 30 credits each.
- Tick **Multi page** for a sidebar or tabs with a page behind each button,
  every page written for its name. Name the pages you want in the prompt, up to
  ten. It costs 30 credits for the first page and 5 for each one after.
- Ask for a form ("the player types a reason and picks a length") and it adds
  [inputs](#inputs).
- Numbers on a generated dialog (balances, prices, player counts) are samples
  for you to change.

<Callout title="It names the buttons; you wire them">
  The AI never invents a command. Buttons arrive named and described, with no
  action, because a command does real things on your server. The exceptions
  are controls bound to a [player setting](#player-settings) and buttons that
  turn [pages](#pages), which are wired for you.
</Callout>

## The editor

The screen sits in the middle, with the **Components** panel on the left and
the selected piece's settings on the right. It behaves like the game: the mouse
wheel scrolls, hovering shows tooltips, and **Clickable areas** (`A`) shades
where a click will land.

**It is the size your game makes it.** Set your **Monitor** and **GUI scale**
in the bar above the screen (the same as *Options › Video Settings › GUI
Scale*) and the editor works out the screen the way Minecraft does, so you see
whether the dialog fits or scrolls. **Game size**, at the bottom of the tools,
shows it at real pixel size.

**Adding.** **+** (or `N`) opens **Add component**, with five shelves and a
search box across all of them:

| Shelf | What is on it |
| --- | --- |
| **Blocks** | Ready-made groups: settings card, setting row, sidebar (plain, of cards or of buttons), tab bar, button stack, card list, profile card, stat cards, product grid, yes or no, header bar, and a [List](#lists-the-server-fills). |
| **Decor** | Banner, hero item, rarity card, ribbon, ornament and glow dividers, window, fade card, big lettering, sparkles and confetti. See [The look](#the-look). |
| **Items** | Item slot, grid of slots, your inventory. See [Items and inventories](#items-and-inventories). |
| **Controls** | Switch, segmented, slider, dropdown, checkbox, stepper, search box, text box, sidebar button, icon button, theme button, list option, badge. |
| **Basics** | Text, heading, big title, button, link, icon, player head, pack glyph, chip, check line, label and value, progress bar, page of text, QR code, divider, space, an image of your own, and a drawing. |
| **Layout** | Row, column, grid, card, plain card, tray. |

The piece follows your pointer and a yellow bar shows where it will land. Click
to place it, hold **Shift** to keep placing, or press **Enter** to put it after
the selection.

**Editing.** Click a piece on the screen or in **Components**. Its panel lists
its words first, then what clicking it does and its tooltip, then its look.
Double-click any words on the screen to type straight onto them. Drag the blue
grips to resize, and double-click a grip to go back to fitting the content. The
bar under the screen (**Screen › Card › Row › Button**) selects any parent in
one click.

**Moving.** Drag a piece to move it between rows and columns. Hold **Shift**
while dragging, or use **Place anywhere**, to lift it out of the layout and put
it at any pixel; it snaps to edges and centres (hold **Alt** not to). **Back
into the layout** returns it. With one placed piece selected, **Ctrl**-click
others to select them too: the panel lines them up by their edges or middles, or
spreads them with even gaps, and the arrow keys and Delete act on all of them.

**Shortcuts.** Right-click a piece for **Edit words**, copy, paste, duplicate,
**Group in a column**, **Put on a panel**, **Ungroup** and delete, or use
`Ctrl+C`, `Ctrl+X`, `Ctrl+V`, `Ctrl+D`, `Ctrl+G` and `Ctrl+Shift+G`. Pieces
paste into your other dialogs too, even in another tab. `Ctrl+Z` undoes and `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or
`Ctrl+Y` redoes. Changes save on their own.

**The dialog's own settings.** Click the empty space around the screen (or
press Escape) for: **Title** (and whether the game shows it), **Players open it
with**, the live values it uses, **Content width** (160 to 388 pixels, with a
note on which screens it fits), **Theme**, tooltip style, **Click sound**,
**Footer** (**One button**, **Yes / No** or **None**), **Inputs**, and
**Behaviour** (**Escape closes it**, **Pauses singleplayer** and [After a
click](#after-a-click)).

**JSON** shows the layout, and its **minecraft:dialog** tab shows the file the
game reads. **Export** downloads the dialog for a plugin, a datapack or as a
picture (see [Getting it onto a server](#getting-it-onto-a-server)), and, for a
dialog with [motion](#motion), as an **Animated GIF** for a store page or a post.

**Edit with AI**, on the page strip, changes what is on a page. Its **Restyle**
tab changes only how it looks: say "dark luxury with gold" or "a cosy winter
cabin" and it picks a new kit, colours, banner scene, lettering, glows and
motion, leaving every word, button and click exactly as it was. Either costs 15
AI Credits and `Ctrl+Z` takes it back.

**Export as PNG**, on a piece's right-click menu, saves that piece as images for
dialog plugins that build screens from pictures: the art without words, a lit
version for buttons, a `.slice` file giving the columns that must not stretch,
and previews with the words drawn on.

## The pieces

A dialog is up to 388 pixels wide and about 1,000 tall, built from rows,
columns and grids. When it is taller than the screen, the game scrolls it.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Button** | The game's grey button. **Selected** makes it gold, **Greyed out** disables it, **Words from the left** starts its label at the left edge rather than the middle (for a sidebar of buttons), and it can take its own colour. Its panel can also make it the theme's button or the GUI's light one. Its icon can be [a picture of your own](#pictures-in-buttons-and-bars). |
| **Link** | Clickable words, for a contents list. |
| **Text**, **Heading**, **Big title** | Words in the game's font, or [one of your own](#your-own-fonts). Colour codes work: `&a` green, `&c` red, `&6` gold, `&e` yellow, `&b` aqua, `&7` grey, `&l` bold, `&r` reset, `&#55ff55` any colour. |
| **Page of text** | `# Title`, `## Heading`, `### Heading` (gold), `- bullets`, `> notes`, `**gold words**`, `---` for a rule. |
| **Icon** | Any vanilla item, a player head, your pack's own sprites and textures, one of more than a hundred drawn icons (the **Small** shelf of the picker has nine-pixel ones for labels), or one of your own pictures (the **Pictures** shelf). Bare or in a slot. |
| **Image** | A PNG, JPG, GIF (first frame) or WebP up to 512 pixels a side. Add it from **Add component** or drop the file on the screen. It is kept with the pack for its other dialogs. To put a picture *behind* other pieces, give their box a [Picture](#a-boxs-own-look). |
| **Drawing** | A picture you draw in the editor, with the same tools and colours as the texture editor. **Add component** › **Drawing** starts a blank one as wide as the dialog; set its size on the right, one pixel per pixel of the dialog, and press **Done** to place it. Every text, rectangle, ellipse, line and paste lands on a layer of its own, and the **Move** tool (V) picks any of them by clicking it and drags it; the arrows nudge it and Delete removes it. Text stays editable: its words, font (Minecraft, Classic, Small Caps, Tiny, Block or your pack's own), size, colour or fade or rainbow, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, shadow and outline, alignment and spacing. **Turn into pixels** lets you paint on it. **Draw on it**, in any image's panel, opens a drawing again with its layers as you left them. |
| **Chip**, **Badge**, **Check line**, **Label and value** | A balance or tag; a small solid tag such as NEW; a ✔ or ✘ line; a label left and a value right. |
| **Progress bar** | A label, a count and a bar, in its own **Bar colour** and **Track colour** if you like. **Icon before the label** puts a small icon at the start of its line. It can [follow the player](#bars-that-follow-the-player). |
| **Controls** | Switch, segmented (two to four choices), slider, dropdown, checkbox, stepper, search box and text box, drawn in the theme. They are pictures unless bound to a [player setting](#player-settings); players can [type in a text box](#typing-in-a-drawn-box). |
| **Sidebar button**, **List option**, **Icon button**, **Theme button** | The parts of a settings screen. A sidebar button can be taller than its 18 pixels, carry a badge, and show [a picture of your own](#pictures-in-buttons-and-bars). |
| **Item slots** | See [Items and inventories](#items-and-inventories). |
| **QR code**, **Divider**, **Space** | A QR code opens its link when clicked. |

Any box can sit on a surface, chosen under **Behind it**: the theme's **Card**
(with or without a **Heading band**), a sunk **Tray**, a highlight **Band**
behind one row, the GUI's light **Panel** or **Dark panel**, an item **Slot**,
or the see-through **Shade**, **Deep shade** and **Gold**. Tick **Clicking** on a
row or column to make the whole box one button.

**Themes.** Cards, controls and highlights are drawn in the dialog's colours.
Pick one of fourteen under **Colours** (Stone, Slate, Deepslate, Nether, Ocean,
Jungle, End, Royal, Oak, Parchment, Ice, Cherry, Redstone, Sand), or choose any
**Card** and **Accent** colour and the rest is worked out from those. What
they are drawn *with* is the [kit](#kits).

### Pictures in buttons and bars

Anything with an icon can show one of your own pictures instead: a button, a
sidebar button, a tab, a chip in a header bar, a card heading. In its **Icon**
picker, open **Pictures** and choose one, or **Import a picture…**. The picture
is fitted whole into the icon with its shape kept: a small one is scaled up by
whole steps so pixel art stays sharp, and a big one is shrunk smoothly.

An icon is 16 pixels, which is small for a picture. A button or a sidebar
button can draw it bigger: set **Icon size** in its panel to 24, 32 or 48 and
the button grows to hold it. A sidebar of picture buttons is a
**Sidebar** from **Blocks** with a picture and a size on each entry.

For a picture *beside* the buttons, such as a logo at the left of a top bar, drop
an **Image** into the bar's row. For one *behind* a whole bar, give the bar a
[Picture](#a-boxs-own-look).

### Your own fonts

A text piece can be set in a font of your own: under **Font** in its panel,
choose **Upload a font (.ttf)**, or pick one the pack already has. Fonts are
kept with the pack and shared with its GUIs.

The words are drawn into the dialog's picture, so the font never ships and
works on every server and engine. A pixel font is drawn exactly at its own size
(which the editor detects) and at 2x or 3x; any other font is drawn at the size
you choose, best at 10px or more, lined up with the pixels so its strokes stay
even. A character the font doesn't have is drawn as its other case, or else in
Minecraft's own font, so a font for another script (a Thai font with no English
letters, say) works too, its marks drawn over the letter they belong to. Scripts
written right to left, or whose letters join up (Arabic, Hindi), are not drawn
correctly. Colour codes still work. **Bold** is only what you tick:
a heading keeps the font's own weight.

Words holding a [live value](#live-values) are drawn by the game, so they stay
in its font. Fonts can be TrueType (`.ttf`) or a collection (`.ttc`), up to 2
MB; an `.otf` whose letters are PostScript curves cannot be read, so use the
font's `.ttf` version.

### Tooltips

Anything clickable can have a tooltip, written as plain lines:

```
Tuesday - Free

Reward
∙ 250 Coins

Click to claim!
```

The first line is the title. A line above a list of `∙` items is its heading,
lines starting ✔ are green, ⌀ or ✘ red, and "Click" yellow. Colour codes work
too. The dialog's tooltip setting changes the box, rim and colours for the
whole dialog, or takes them **From the theme**. A footer button's tooltip always
keeps the game's box, and a slot of the player's own inventory shows the
item's real tooltip.

### What a click can do

Set under **When clicked** on any clickable piece or footer button:

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Run command** | The player runs it, with their own permissions. |
| **Run as console** | The server runs it as the console, so the player needs no permission for it. `{player}` is who clicked. Needs RP Engine 0.2.39 or newer, or Dialog Engine 0.6.6 or newer. |
| **Suggest command** | A small box opens with the command typed in; they finish it and press **Run**. (Minecraft does not let a dialog fill the chat box.) |
| **Open link** | The game asks before opening it. Must start with `http://` or `https://`. |
| **Copy text** | Copies it to their clipboard. |
| **Go to page** | Turns to another [page](#pages), or makes a new one. |
| **Close the dialog** | Closes it, for an X or a Cancel you drew yourself. |
| **Open another dialog** | Opens one of the pack's dialogs straight away. **Another id…** takes any dialog id the server already knows. On Dialog Engine 0.6.1 and newer that includes Dialog Engine's own dialogs, by their id (`shop:main`) or by the name of one in the same folder (`main`). |
| **Nothing** | Only shows the tooltip. On a footer button, it closes the dialog. |

**Run command** runs as the player, so they need permission for it, and they
could just as well type it in chat. **Run as console** is for a command only
the dialog should give them: `warp spawn {player}` from a menu, without
everybody having `/warp`. The command never reaches the player's game (the
button only names it), and the server runs it only for somebody whose dialog
has that button on it, so typing it in chat does nothing. It cannot use what
the player typed into a text box, which would let them write the console's
command for it.

Minecraft refuses a whole dialog if one action is invalid, so a button with a
bad link, a bad id or an empty command is sent with no action, and the editor
marks it until you fix it.

The game finds a click by the 9-pixel line of text it lands on, so a clickable
piece is clickable on every line it covers at least half of. **Clickable
areas** shows the real shape.

### Inputs

A dialog can ask for something with Minecraft's own text box, checkbox, list or
slider, drawn under the screen and above the footer. Add them under **Inputs**
in the dialog's settings. Each has a **Key**, and a footer button's command
can use `$(key)`:

```
ban {target} $(length) $(reason)
```

A checkbox gives `true` or `false`, and a choice written `7d=7 days` shows
"7 days" and gives `7d`. Only footer buttons fill in keys.

### Typing in a drawn box

A **Text box** from the **Controls** shelf is a picture of a box until you
switch on **Typing** in its panel. Then players click it and type in it, and
what they typed reaches a footer button's command under the key in **Saved
as**, like any input:

```
block $(player)
```

Minecraft only takes typing in its own text box, so Studio sets the game's box
exactly on your drawn one and the pack hides the game's black look of it. What
players type is written by the game, in light grey from the box's left edge,
so the box draws no words of its own. A dark theme reads best.

Where the game puts its box is the game's choice, and the editor follows it:

- **The box is always centred.** A text box as wide as its card lines up by
  itself. One off to the side is marked in amber with where the game will put
  it; **Line it up** in its panel moves it there.
- **A second box goes 10 pixels under the first**, a third 10 under that.
- **The first box ends the clickable part of the dialog.** Nothing beside or
  below it can be clicked, so the button that sends the answer goes in the
  footer. Words and pictures below it are still drawn.
- **Leave Longest answer empty** unless you need a limit. A box takes as much
  as fits in it; with a limit, the game shows a count such as `3/16` under the
  box.

It lines up exactly on Minecraft 1.21.11 and newer. On 1.21.6 to 1.21.10 the
game puts its box 4 pixels higher, so what is typed sits at the top of the
drawn box.

## The look

### Kits

A kit is what everything is drawn with: the material the cards are made of,
how round their corners are, their frame, the dividers, how headings are
lettered, and whether lit things glow. Pick one under **Kit** in the dialog's
settings:

| Kit | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Classic** | The plain raised cards, in any colours. |
| **Scroll** | Parchment in a gilded frame, wax-red accents. |
| **Luxe** | Near-black velvet with gold trim and glowing highlights. |
| **Glass** | Dark glass with bright corner brackets and scanlines. |
| **Wood** | Warm planks and cream words. |
| **Frost** | Pale ice with soft corners. |
| **Forge** | Cracked rock with glowing seams. |
| **Arcade** | Black with neon outlines. |
| **Meadow** | Leafy green and sunshine gold. |
| **Castle** | Grey stone bricks and gold. |
| **Night** | A starry sky. |

A kit comes in its own colours, and you can change them under **Colours**
without losing the kit. Changing kit never moves anything: every piece keeps
its place and size.

On a dialog with [pages](#pages), each page can have a kit and colours of its
own. **Kit and colours for** in the dialog's settings says where a change goes:
**This page** changes only the page you have open, **Every page** changes them
all. While every page shares one look, a change goes to every page; once a page
has its own, it goes to that page only, and **Use this page's look on every
page** puts them all back in one.

### A box's own look

Any row, column or card has a **Look** section in its panel:

- **Picture**: an image behind the box's content, cropped to fill it, with
  **Darken for words** so a title reads over it. Choose one of your images, one
  of the **Starter banners** (free), or **Draw it with AI**: describe a scene
  and the image model paints it at exactly the box's size (10 AI Credits).
- **Fill** in a colour of its own, fading down or across to a second one. Words
  on it take colours that read on it.
- **Made of**: planks, parchment, stone, bricks, velvet, glass, frost, magma,
  leaves, metal, stars or grid.
- **Corners**, **Border** (thin or thick), **Bevel**, **Drop shadow**, **Glow**,
  **Corner ornaments** (gold, brackets or rivets) and **Opacity**.

A **Banner** from the **Decor** shelf is a box like this, ready-made: a picture
across the top with its title lettered over it, a line under it and a chip at
its right. Its art prompt is already in its panel, ready to draw.

### Lettering, glows and decorations

- **Lettering** on a text piece: an **Outline**, letters that **Fade** to a
  second colour, and a **Shadow** colour or none. **Huge** and **Giant** sizes
  draw words three and four times their size.
- An icon's **Glow** puts a soft halo behind it, and **Burst of rays** a ring of
  rays: for the thing to look at.
- A badge can be a **Ribbon** with notched ends. A divider can be an
  **Ornament**, **Glow**, **Fade** or **Dots** line.
- A **Decoration** scatters sparkles, stars, confetti or hearts over its box.
  Place it anywhere, over a banner or round a reward.

These are drawn into the dialog's picture, so they need nothing on the server.
Words that hold a [live value](#live-values) are drawn by the game, so they keep
its font and take no lettering.

### Motion

Any piece can **shimmer** (a shine sweeps across it), **pulse**, **twinkle**,
cycle through the **rainbow** or **flicker** like candlelight, slowly, normally
or fast. Set it under **Motion** in the piece's panel; everything inside the
piece moves with it. The editor plays it as it will look, and the pause button
on the left stops it while you work.

In game it is played by your resource pack, so it needs nothing from the
plugin: a player with the pack sees it move, from the moment the dialog opens.
The parts the game draws itself (live words, player heads, inventory items)
stay still.

### Text effects on words

If your pack has [text effects](/text-effects), a text piece's
**Text effect** puts one on its words: a wave, a rainbow, fire. The game draws
those words, in its own font, and your pack's shader plays the effect.

### A Close in the dialog's style

The game's grey footer button looks the same on every dialog. **Draw it in the
dialog's kit instead**, under **Footer**, hides it and puts a button that
closes the dialog just under the content, which you can move and restyle.
Generated dialogs in a kit, and most templates, start that way.

## Getting it onto a server

**Paired with RP Engine**: push the pack and open the dialog with `/rp dialog
<id>`. It opens straight away, no restart needed. RP Engine also writes the
dialogs into a datapack, so after the next restart each is a real
`namespace:id` you can open from a command block or your own datapack.

**As files**: the editor's **Export** menu downloads the dialog for a server
that isn't paired:

| Export | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| **Dialog Engine (.zip)** | Unzip into `plugins/`, run `/de reload`, open with `/de open <pack>:<dialog>`. `/de edit` brings it back into Studio's editor. |
| **RP Engine (.zip)** | A content folder. Unzip into `plugins/` and run `/rp reload`. |
| **Datapack (.zip)** | Every dialog in the pack as `minecraft:dialog` files. See [Without RP Engine](#without-rp-engine). |
| **Resource pack (.zip)** | Only the pictures and fonts this dialog uses. |
| **Picture (.png)** | The screen at three times its size. |

Both plugin downloads carry everything the plugin fills in: live values,
inventories, bars, lists, pages and conditions.

### A command of its own

`/rp dialog` is for staff. To let players open a dialog themselves, give it a
command under **Players open it with**, such as `shop`. Everybody can use it,
or only players with a permission you name. `/shop Steve` opens it about
Steve, as `{target}`. If another plugin already owns the command, that plugin
keeps it.

### A dialog about somebody

A punish menu is about the player being punished. Write `{target}` (or any
name) in the title, words, tooltips or commands, and give its value when you
open it:

```
/rp dialog studio:punish target=Steve         # open it for yourself
/rp dialog studio:punish target=Steve Admin   # open it for Admin, about Steve
```

The player to open it for goes last, and opening it for someone else needs
`rpengine.dialog.others`. Plugins pass values through [RP Engine's
API](/rp-engine/api#dialogs).

### The Quick Actions key

Minecraft 1.21.6 added a Quick Actions key, **G** unless a player rebinds it.
Tick **Open it with the Quick Actions key** in the dialog's panel and G opens
it. Put several dialogs on the key and G shows a list of them to pick from.

The key is the player's game, not the server: pressing it opens a copy of the
dialog the player was sent when they joined, and the server is never told. That
gives you two ways to put a dialog on it:

- **Opens the dialog** (the default). G opens the dialog straight away. RP
  Engine (or Dialog Engine) draws each player's copy for them as they join, so their name, head and
  any other value shows what was true at that moment, and stays that way until
  they join again. Buttons in it work as usual. This needs Paper. On Spigot, and
  for a dialog behind a permission (which is not known yet while a player is
  joining), G opens the launcher instead.
- **Opens a launcher**. G opens a small screen with an **Open** button, and the
  button has the server open the dialog, drawn fresh like any other. One extra
  click, and everything in it is up to date. Use this when a dialog shows
  something that changes, like a balance or a rank.

A dialog with the player's own items in it always uses the launcher. Either way,
the key only picks up a change when the server **restarts**: what G opens is
part of the data the server reads as it starts, not of the resource pack, so
nothing extra is downloaded.

### Without RP Engine

A dialog is a vanilla `minecraft:dialog`, so a datapack alone can run one. Put
the **Datapack** export in your world's `datapacks/` folder, restart, and open
it with `/dialog show @s studio:<id>`. Players still need the resource pack,
either the whole pack or the dialog's own **Resource pack** export. To add that
to a pack of your own, load it as a separate pack, or copy its files in and
merge any `assets/minecraft/font/default.json` into yours rather than
overwriting it. Its `README.txt` lists which character is which picture.

The **Datapack** export puts a dialog that is on the Quick Actions key on the
key as well, and G opens it as written.

Without a plugin, everything per player is missing: live values show as
written, bound controls stay on their default, inventories, live heads, bars and
lists are empty, conditions are ignored, and `/rp` commands do nothing. Page
turns and **Close the dialog** still work. The **minecraft:dialog** tab lists
which of these a dialog uses.

## Advanced features

Everything below is filled in by the plugin as the dialog opens, so it needs RP
Engine or Dialog Engine on the server.

### Live values

Write a name in curly braces, like `{player}`, and it is filled in for each
player: `Coins: {coins}` shows their balance, `Punish {target}` names the
player the menu is about. Live values work in the title, the words on the
screen, tooltips, footer buttons and the commands clicks run. Select some words
and open **Insert a live value** to pick one.

| Placeholder | What it is |
|---|---|
| `{player}`, `{displayname}` | Their name, and their name with any prefix |
| `{health}`, `{health_max}`, `{health_percent}` | Health |
| `{food}` | Hunger, out of 20 |
| `{level}`, `{xp}` | Experience level, and percent to the next |
| `{xp_points}`, `{xp_needed}` | Points into this level, and what the level takes |
| `{air}` | Seconds of breath left |
| `{ping}` | Latency in milliseconds |
| `{world}`, `{x}`, `{y}`, `{z}`, `{direction}` | Where they are |
| `{time}`, `{day}` | The world's clock and day count |
| `{gamemode}` | Their game mode |
| `{online}`, `{max_online}` | Players online, and the cap |
| `{uuid}` | Their UUID |

Any other name works too. The first of these that has a value wins:

1. Values the dialog was opened with (`name=value` on `/rp dialog`, or from a
   plugin).
2. The player's own [settings](#player-settings).
3. Values a plugin set for the player with `overlays().set(...)`, the same ones
   [shader object](/shaders#live-values) labels show.
4. The built-in list above.
5. PlaceholderAPI, if installed: `{vault_eco_balance}` or `%vault_eco_balance%`.
   Names are letters, digits and `_` only.

A name nothing answers is shown as written. A placeholder can choose between
words: `{show_sidebar?on:Shown|off:Hidden}`, where the last choice is used when
no other matches.

Live words stay on one line (press Enter in them for a new line), and a value
too long for its space is cut short with "…". Characters the game's small font
cannot draw, such as Chinese or emoji, show as "?".

**A player's face.** In a **Player head**'s icon picker, choose **Whoever opens
it** (`head:{player}`) or **Who it is about** (`head:{target}`), and the game
draws that player's own skin. This needs Minecraft 1.21.9 or newer; the editor
shows Steve. A head given a username instead is fixed to that player's skin at
the time you saved.

### Player settings

A switch, segmented control, slider, stepper, checkbox or dropdown is normally
just a picture. Type a name under **Player setting** in its panel (lower case,
like `show_sidebar`) and it shows each player's own setting instead. Clicking it
changes their setting and redraws the dialog. Settings are kept per player
across restarts, with no setup.

A dropdown opens into a list of its options when clicked (upward, if it is near
the bottom of the dialog), and picking one sets it and closes the list.
Clicking the dropdown again, or any other control, closes the list without
picking.
A slider moves its knob to wherever you click on its track; its arrows step it
one position at a time.

A slider or stepper can show a setting with at most 21 positions, so 0 to 100
needs a step of 5 or more. With more than that it stays a picture, and its
panel says so and suggests a step.

A setting with more options than fit on one segmented control can be split
across several: give each the same **Player setting**. Each one lights up only
when the setting is one of its own options, and shows nothing chosen otherwise,
so a choice of eight names can be two rows of four. A player who has never
picked one gets the first control's default. Only segmented controls share a
setting this way (not the cycle look); for any other control, the first one
with that name is the one that works.

Other plugins read a setting as `%rpengine_var_show_sidebar%` through
PlaceholderAPI, or through [RP Engine's API](/rp-engine/api#player-settings).
Inside the dialog, `{show_sidebar}` is its value. A player can only set a
setting to a value some dialog on the server offers.

### Bars that follow the player

A **Progress bar**'s value and most can be live values, so it shows each
player's own progress. The **Follow** shortcuts set both at once:

| Follow | Value | Most |
|---|---|---|
| Hearts | `{health}` | `{health_max}` |
| Hunger | `{food}` | `20` |
| Progress to the next level | `{xp}` | `100` |
| Experience points | `{xp_points}` | `{xp_needed}` |
| Breath underwater | `{air}` | `15` |
| Health, as a percentage | `{health_percent}` | `100` |

Any value works, such as `{kills}` out of `100`. Commas and `%` are ignored, so
`1,250` and `75%` read fine, but a value that is not a plain number (including
`1.2k`) leaves the bar empty. The editor draws a live bar 60% full.

### Lists the server fills

A **List** (on the Blocks shelf) is one row repeated for each item the server
gives it, up to twelve: the player's homes, the warps, friends online. In the
row, `{warps}` is each row's own value and `{warps_note}` a second field of it.
Empty rows are hidden, but their space is kept, so put a list at the end of a
card. Fill it when opening:

```
/rp dialog studio:warps warps=Spawn,Shop,Arena
```

or from a plugin with `dialogs().list(...)` or `listRows(...)` (see [the Plugin
API](/rp-engine/api#dialogs)), with each row's button running, for example,
`warp {warps}`.

Switch the list to **Repeated a number of times** when the server knows how
many but not what each is called. **How many** can be a number, or a live value
such as `{home_slots}`. Then the list's own name is the row's number: a button
`Home {home}` running `home {home}` reads *Home 1*, *Home 2* and so on.

### Pages

A dialog can have up to 99 pages, for a settings screen with a sidebar, a
store's categories or a guide's chapters.

The quickest way: select a sidebar button, and its panel offers **A page for
every button**. **Create pages** gives each button its own page. The box beside
the sidebar becomes the part that changes from page to page (outlined in
green), and everything outside it stays the same on every page.

- The **Pages** strip above the screen has a tab per page. Double-click to
  rename; the first tab is where the dialog opens. **+** adds a copy of the
  current page.
- Edits outside the green outlines change every page; edits inside one change
  only this page. Tick **Changes from page to page** on more boxes, such as the
  info under a sidebar as well as the content beside it, and each changes from
  page to page on its own.
- A button that goes to a page lights up on that page by itself.
- **Show on pages**, in any piece's panel, leaves it off the pages you untick,
  even outside the green outline: a balance in the sidebar that the home page
  already shows elsewhere. Its space stays empty there, so nothing else moves
  when the page turns.
- **Edit with AI**, at the right of the strip, redraws the page you are on from
  a description, either only its own part (when it has one) or the whole
  screen. Each edit costs
  15 AI Credits, and `Ctrl+Z` takes it back.

In game each page is its own dialog: `studio:settings`, then
`studio:settings.audio` and so on. A dialog with pages stays open when clicked,
so pages turn without flashing the world, and keep the values the first page
was opened with. After a server restart, pages with nothing per player on them
turn instantly; `/rp dialogs` says which.

### Items and inventories

The **Items** shelf has an **Item slot**, a **Grid of slots** (up to 13 by 8)
and **Your inventory**. Hovering an item shows the game's own tooltip. Set what
a grid holds under **What is in it**, and its slot look (inventory, dark well or
theme) and **Size** (1×, 2×, 3×) in its panel.

**Items you choose.** Pick a slot, then search any Minecraft item or one of your
pack's textures, and set its count, name, lore, rarity and enchanted look.
**When clicked** works per slot, so each item in a shop can run its own `buy`
command. The panel marks items newer than 1.21.6, whose tooltips RP Engine
leaves out on an older server so the dialog still opens.

**Items from your server's own pack.** A custom item from ItemsAdder, Nexo,
Oraxen or a resource pack of your own works too. Pick the item it is made from
(often Paper), then under **Your server's own item** give it its **Item model**
(like `nexo:ruby`) and its **Custom model data**: numbers, strings, flags or
colours, with commas between several. A plugin's custom model data number, like
`1001`, goes in **Numbers**. The tooltip then shows that exact item. Studio can't
see your server's pack, so choose a **Picture in the slot**: your item's texture
or any picture of it, fitted to the slot. Without one, the slot shows a question
mark, the same as a player's inventory shows that item.

**The player's inventory.** A grid can show the inventory, the hotbar, or the
ender chest of whoever opens it, with their real items and tooltips. Your pack's
own items show as themselves; items from other plugins show as a question mark
with their real tooltip. On Spigot (rather than Paper), tooltips show only name
and lore.

**Moving items.** Tick **Players can move items** and players can click a stack
to pick it up, then click another slot to put it down. Moves work between any
movable grids in the dialog, so an ender chest grid over an inventory grid is a
working ender chest. The server checks the real slots on every click, so nothing
can be duplicated. There is no dragging, shift-click or right-click, and keep
such a dialog short enough not to scroll, since it redraws from the top.
Plugins can refuse a move with `DialogSlotMoveEvent`.

**Other storage.** A grid can also show:

- **The item it opened from**, like a backpack. Give an item in your server's
  content folder the `dialog:` action, and its contents are stored on the item
  itself:

  ```yaml
  supply_crate:
    material: PAPER
    model: supply_crate
    actions:
      right_click:
        - dialog: studio:supply_crate
        - cancel: true
  ```

- **Storage kept on the player** under a name you choose, such as a vault.
- **A container another plugin registers** with RP Engine.

### Show when

Under **Show when** in any piece's panel, add conditions: a [player
setting](#player-settings) or live value compared with something (*is*, *is
not*, *is more than* and so on), or a permission the player has or lacks (such
as `group.vip` for a LuckPerms group). Choose whether **all** or **any one** must
hold. Players they don't hold for see empty space, and the piece's clicks are
gone too.

**Show something else instead…** puts a stand-in in its place for everyone else,
such as a greyed **VIP only** button. The two share a **Show one of** box
(up to six pieces), and each player sees the first whose condition holds.

**Colour when** keeps the piece for everyone but changes its colours for some
players: a talent-tree button that turns green from level 4. The first matching
look wins.

These are also how a plugin changes a button for one player. **Selected** and
**Greyed out** are part of the picture, so a plugin can't flip them while the
dialog is open: draw the button both ways and let **Show something else
instead…** choose, on a [live value](#live-values) the plugin sets.

The editor shows the dialog as a player with the default settings sees it, with
every permission held. In **Components**, a crossed-out eye marks a piece with
conditions, and a palette marks one with looks.

### Click sounds

Clicks on the drawn screen make no sound in the game itself, so the plugin
plays one. **Click sound** in the dialog's settings is **The game's click** (with
pitch and volume), **Another sound** (one of your pack's or the game's), or
**Silent**. Any clickable piece can override it with **Its own**. Clicks that
never reach the server (links, copy, pages that turn instantly) make no sound,
and footer buttons always make the game's click. The server-wide default is
`dialogs.click-sound` in RP Engine's [configuration](/rp-engine/config#dialogs)
and `click-sound` in Dialog Engine's.

### After a click

Under **Behaviour**, choose what the dialog does once something is clicked:

| Choice | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Seamless** (default) | Stays on screen until the server answers. A dialog the click opens replaces it without a flash, and otherwise it closes. In a datapack it acts as **Close**. |
| **Close** | Closes at once, so the next dialog arrives after a glimpse of the world. |
| **Stay open** | Stays as it is. |
| **Wait** | Shows the game's "Waiting for Server" screen. A dialog with a link or copy button closes instead. |

A seamless dialog stays open after a click the server never hears, such as a
link or a `/server` command on a proxy. Escape closes it. Dialogs with pages
always stay open.

### Icons from your server's pack

If your server already has font icons from ItemsAdder, Nexo, Oraxen or a pack of
your own, add a **Pack glyph** (Basics) and paste the icon in as a text
component (`{"text":"","font":"minecraft:default"}`), MiniMessage
(`<font:nexo:default></font>`) or the character itself. Give its **Height**
and **Ascent** from the font's JSON, and its **Width** (the picture's width ×
height ÷ the picture's height). A wrong width shifts whatever follows on the
line. Studio can't see your pack, so the glyph is a marked box in the editor;
choose a **Picture in the editor** to see it while you lay it out. Only players
with your pack see the icon, and it moves up and down in 9-pixel steps.

A [live value](#live-values) works in a pasted glyph too, so a placeholder can
choose the glyph for each player: `{"text":"%card_face%","font":"cards:pixels"}`
shows whatever `%card_face%` returns, in that font. With RP Engine 0.2.34 or
Dialog Engine 0.6.4, the colour codes in the value are kept (`§a`,
`§x§f§f§8§8§0§0`, `&a` and `&#ff8800`), so a placeholder that colours its glyphs
for each player, such as a face built from one-pixel glyphs, looks the way it
does in chat. Older versions show the glyphs white. The glyph's place is as
wide as you set, so set **Width** to what the value draws.

With RP Engine 0.2.10 or newer you can also paste an **RP Engine icon** the
way chat writes it: `:mypack:thirst:`, `:thirst:` or `<mypack:thirst>`. RP
Engine puts the icon in as the dialog opens and measures it, so its width
can't be off. A [live value](#live-values) can pick the icon for each player:
`:status:{thirst_state}:` shows whichever icon that value names. Anywhere
else, including Dialog Engine and an older RP Engine, its place is left blank.

## Plugin versions

Dialogs open with any RP Engine that supports them, but these features need at
least:

| Feature | RP Engine |
| --- | --- |
| Player settings | 0.1.62 |
| Pages | 0.1.66 (instant page turns: 0.1.70) |
| Live words that stay inside their line, live heads | 0.1.69 |
| Inventories and storage, lists, a command of its own | 0.1.74 |
| Bars that follow the player | 0.1.75 |
| Show when, Show one of, Colour when, repeated rows | 0.2.2 |
| Click sounds | 0.2.5 |
| Seamless after a click | 0.2.6 |
| RP Engine icons in a Pack glyph | 0.2.10 |
| Colour codes from a placeholder in a Pack glyph | 0.2.34 |
| The Quick Actions key | 0.2.17 |

On an older RP Engine the feature fails quietly: a bound control shows every
state at once, a live bar is empty, a conditioned piece is drawn for everybody,
and so on. In Dialog Engine, click sounds need a version newer than 0.3.5,
Seamless needs 0.4.0, the Quick Actions key 0.6.0 (and opening a drawn
dialog straight from it, rather than through the launcher, 0.6.5), and colour codes from a placeholder in a Pack glyph
0.6.4.

## Older UI-style dialogs

Dialogs made before 30 September 2026 may be in the older UI style: a drawn
256×256 picture with button guides, edited in the GUI editor. They still open,
edit and sync as before, but new dialogs aren't made this way.

- **Button guides** are the dashed boxes where the game draws each button.
  Paint inside one to give the button a face; leave it empty for the game's grey
  button.
- **Clickable areas**: pick the **Areas** tool (`A`) and drag a box over any part
  of the picture to give it an action and a tooltip.
- **Size**: the **In game** box under Size says which screens it fits, and
  **Shrink to fit every screen** makes it fit a 240-pixel-tall screen.
- **Nudge**: if the picture sits a few pixels off in game, type an offset in the
  **Nudge** fields, save and look again.
- A UI-style dialog saved before 26 September 2026 only answers clicks on its
  first line in 1.21.9 and newer. Open it, save, and sync again.
