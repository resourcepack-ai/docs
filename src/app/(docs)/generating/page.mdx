# Generating with AI

Generation exists to get you past the blank grid. What comes back won't
always be perfect, and that's fine: the editors are there for the finishing
touches.

## Where it lives

Everything starts from **Add asset** on the pack page. First you choose how to
start (**Generate**, **Draw** or **Upload**), then what you're making, and
the fields underneath change with the type: which vanilla block or item you're
replacing, a resolution for pixel art, an event name for a sound.

Not every type is behind every option:

| | Generate | Draw | Upload |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Block** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ `.png` `.jpg` `.webp` |
| **Model** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ `.json` `.bbmodel` |
| **Item** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ `.png` `.jpg` `.webp` |
| **GUI** | ✕ | ✓ | ✕ |
| **Icon** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ `.png` `.jpg` `.webp` |
| **Sound** | ✓ | ✕ | ✓ `.ogg` |

**GUIs can't be generated yet**: the AI isn't capable of drawing a good
screen, and we're working on it. **Sounds can't be drawn**, since there's
nothing to draw.

## Reference images

Blocks, items, models and icons can take a **reference image** alongside the
prompt: drop a picture on the Generate form and the AI
works from it as well as your words. A model built from a reference picks up
its shape and palette, which is often the shortest route to "like this, but
Minecraft".

## Writing a prompt

Be specific about **material, colour and structure**. Be vague about pixels:
the model can't count them for you, and asking it to will cost you the parts
it's good at.

| Good | Bad | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *weathered copper roof tiles, blue-green patina, strong diagonal grain* | *a nice block, 4px border, dark at the bottom, make it pop* | Material and wear beat pixel instructions and "make it pop" |
| *oak market stall, striped red canopy, crate of apples on the counter* | *a detailed medieval shop with lots of decorations* | Named parts give the builder something to build; "detailed" doesn't |
| *golden key item, ornate bow, three teeth* | *cool key* | The parts you name are the parts you get |
| *heavy iron door slamming, echo in a stone hall* | *scary sound* | Describe what's making the noise and where it happens |
| *a red potion bottle glyph, thick dark outline* | *tiny detailed potion icon with sparkles and reflections* | At glyph sizes, detail turns to noise; silhouette first |

Some things worth naming explicitly:

- **The material.** "Oxidised copper" gets you further than "green metal".
- **The wear.** New, weathered, cracked, mossy. This is most of what makes a
  texture look like it belongs in Minecraft.
- **The structure.** Planks, tiles, bricks, panels, woven. Give the eye a
  repeating unit.
- **What it sits next to.** "reads next to deepslate" is a real constraint and
  the model will use it.

<Callout type="tip">
  Prompt for the thing, not for the fix. If a texture is close but too busy,
  say "same block, fewer distinct colours, calmer surface" rather than trying
  to describe the finished pixels.
</Callout>

## Iterating

You can send an existing asset back for another pass with a new instruction.
For a texture, that pass sees the current pixel grid, so it edits rather than
starts over. That's usually better than re-rolling the original prompt: you
keep the palette and silhouette you already liked. For a model, **Edit with
AI** is more targeted still: the builder inspects the model's structure
first and changes only what the instruction asks, instead of regenerating
the whole thing.

<Callout type="warning">
  Iterating still costs a generation, and a texture pass can drift. If you've
  painted a texture by hand, duplicate it first: that path edits the whole
  grid, not just the part you mentioned.
</Callout>

## Notifications

A model can take up to ten minutes, and you are not meant to sit and watch
it. Turn on a notification and close the tab: anything that runs longer than
two minutes gets one, and anything faster doesn't, since it lands while
you're still looking at it. In practice that means models.

All three channels live on your
[account page](https://studio.resourcepack.ai/account), under
**Notifications**, and they're independent:

| Channel | Needs |
| --- | --- |
| **Email** | The address on your account, verified |
| **Discord** | Discord connected under Sign-in methods, and you in [our Discord](https://resourcepack.ai/discord) |
| **In game** | Your Minecraft account linked; the message arrives where you're standing on <ServerAddress /> |

<Callout type="warning" title="The Discord DM needs both halves">
  A bot can only message people it shares a server with; that's Discord's
  rule, not ours. Connect Discord *and* join ours, or the switch sits there
  looking on while no message ever arrives. If DMs still aren't landing,
  check **Privacy Settings → Direct Messages** in the server.
</Callout>

You're told about both outcomes, not just the good one: a model that failed
after twenty minutes is exactly the thing you need to hear about, and the
message links you to the Retry button. Failed generations refund their
credits automatically. The in-game message isn't queued if you're offline;
turn on email as well if you want one that waits for you.

## Knowing when to stop generating

The honest rule: once the silhouette and palette are right, switch to the
editor. Hand-fixing eight pixels takes fifteen seconds and gets you exactly
what you meant; three more prompts take two minutes and get you something
slightly different each time.
