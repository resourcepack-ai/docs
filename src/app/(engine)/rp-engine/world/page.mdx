# Recipes, entities and liquids

## Recipes

```yaml
# recipes/gems.yml
ruby_block:
  type: shaped           # the default
  result: mypack:ruby_block
  pattern: ["RRR", "RRR", "RRR"]
  keys:
    R: mypack:ruby       # a space in the pattern is an empty slot

sapphire_from_lapis:
  type: smelting         # blasting | smoking | campfire | stonecutting | shapeless
  result: mypack:sapphire
  ingredient: LAPIS_LAZULI
  experience: 0.5
  time: 100
```

A content id matches that **exact** item — an ordinary diamond will not satisfy
a recipe calling for `mypack:ruby`. A vanilla material matches loosely, the way
a vanilla ingredient should.

A recipe id is not a content id, so a recipe may be called `ruby_block` while an
item is too. `/rp recipes` lists what loaded.

## Entities

A real mob wearing a model.

```yaml
# entities/guards.yml
guard:
  type: ZOMBIE           # choose it for BEHAVIOUR; the looks are replaced
  model: mypack:guard    # an item id, whose model it wears
  name: "&cTemple Guard"
  health: 40
  scale: 1.2
  silent: false
  tags: [temple, boss]
```

It is genuinely a mob: it walks with its own AI, takes damage, drops its loot,
is found by `@e[tag=boss]` and is seen by every other plugin. Pick the `type`
for how you want it to *behave* — a zombie hunts and burns in daylight, a
villager wanders and flees, an armour stand does nothing at all.

The model is a display entity riding the mob, with the vanilla body made
invisible rather than removed, so the hitbox stays where the model looks. A
custom entity never despawns: one that vanished because a player walked away
would leave its model standing there, because removing a mount ejects its
passengers rather than taking them with it.

`/rp spawn mypack:guard` puts one where you are standing.

<Callout type="note" title="This is for mobs YOU define">
  To put a model on a mob something else spawned — a MythicMobs boss, a
  Citizens NPC — see [Models on mobs](/rp-engine/mobs). The difference is who
  owns the entity, and it matters: that page never replaces one.
</Callout>

## Liquids

<Callout type="warning" title="What a custom liquid is, and is not">
  Minecraft has two fluids and a server cannot add a third. A custom liquid is
  **real water or lava, wearing your texture, with your rules applied to whoever
  is in it.** It genuinely behaves like a fluid because it is one — you swim in
  it, boats float, it flows, other plugins see water. The **texture is
  per-pack**, so every water on the server looks the same; and what tells acid
  from ocean is a **volume you mark out**, not the blocks in it.
</Callout>

```yaml
# liquids/acid.yml
acid:
  base: water            # water | lava
  effect: POISON
  amplifier: 1
  damage: 1.0            # per second
  fireproof: false
```

Mark out a pool in game: stand at one corner, `/rp liquid corner`, stand at the
opposite one, `/rp liquid fill mypack:acid`. `/rp liquid clear` removes the pool
you are standing in.

Pools are boxes rather than lists of blocks, because a lake is thousands of
blocks that change shape as it flows — a per-block record would be wrong within
a second of somebody breaking a bank.
