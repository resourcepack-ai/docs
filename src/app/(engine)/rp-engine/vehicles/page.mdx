# Vehicles

A vehicle is a model people can get into and drive: a car, a boat, a
skateboard, a helicopter, a plane. It has up to eight seats, it stays parked
where you left it (through restarts too), and anyone who right-clicks it gets
in.

There are two ways to make one:

- **In Studio**: open any model, press **Vehicle** to make it one, place the
  seats in the vehicle editor and sync. Everything on this page is filled in
  for you.
- **By hand**: a file in `vehicles/` in your content folder. The vehicle wears
  the model of an item you already have.

```yaml
# vehicles/cars.yml
hatchback:
  model: mypack:hatchback_item  # an item id; the vehicle wears its model
  name: "&bHatchback"
  medium: land              # land | water | air
  speed: 18                 # top speed, blocks per second
  acceleration: 7.5         # how quickly it gets there
  turn-speed: 140           # most it swings round, degrees per second
  weight: 14                # 1-100: slower to get going, harder to shove
  hitbox:                   # what players click, in blocks
    width: 1.4              # side to side
    height: 1.2             # up from the base
    length: 3.0             # front to back
  seats:
    - {role: driver,    x: -0.4, y: 0.6, z: 0.6}
    - {role: passenger, x:  0.4, y: 0.6, z: 0.6}
    - {role: passenger, x: -0.4, y: 0.6, z: -0.5}
    - {role: passenger, x:  0.4, y: 0.6, z: -0.5}
  animations:               # optional: which animation plays when
    idle: parked
    moving: drive
  sounds:                   # optional: which sound plays when
    idle: mypack:tickover
    moving: mypack:engine
```

The vehicle's own id (`hatchback` here) can't also be the `model:` item's id,
since that id is already taken by the item.

## Parking and getting in

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/rp vehicle <id>` | Parks one where you stand |
| `/rp vehicle remove` | Removes the nearest one |
| `/rp vehicles` | Lists the vehicles this server has, with how many hidden seats each has |

The vehicle's item parks one too: right-click the ground holding the item
named by `model:` and the vehicle appears there, facing the way you face. The
item is used up (except in creative). That makes a vehicle something a shop can
sell: give a player that item (`/rp give mypack:hatchback_item`) and they park
it themselves.

Right-click the body to take the first free seat, driver's seat first, so
whoever gets in first drives. Right-click a particular seat to take that one.
Sneak gets out, as it does for a boat. With no `hitbox:` the vehicle is a
one-block cube, which is clickable but usually much smaller than it looks.

When someone gets in they are told the controls once, above their hotbar
(`vehicles.mount-hint` in [config.yml](/rp-engine/config#vehicles)).

## Driving one

The controls depend on the server:

| | Paper 1.21.4 and newer | Anything else |
|---|---|---|
| Speed up / slow down | W and S | Right-click speeds up a notch, left-click slows down, then reverses |
| Steer | A and D | Look where you want to go |
| Space | Handbrake (land and water) | |
| Air vehicles | Space climbs, S descends | Climb and dive with your look |

Paper is worth it here: when steering is looking, the driver can't look at
anything except the road.

- **S brakes before it reverses.** It only engages reverse once you have
  stopped. In the air, S only reverses once you are back on the ground.
- **Space is a drift button.** Pull the handbrake in a corner and the rear lets
  go; ease off and get back on the power to recover. Corner harder than the
  tyres can hold and the car runs wide.
- **`jump: true`** makes space jump (about a block and a half) instead of
  braking, for a dirt bike or a skateboard. On water it pops off the surface,
  like a surfboard off a wave.
- **A vehicle only turns while it's moving**, so a parked one keeps its
  heading. `turn-in-place: true` lets a tank, hovercraft or digger pivot on the
  spot. An aircraft in the air always steers.
- The body follows the ground: it tilts on kerbs and slopes, dips when braking,
  squats under power and bounces on landing. Hills slow you going up. A wall
  hit at an angle scrapes you along it; one hit square stops you.

## Handling reference

| Field | Default | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `medium` | `land` | `land`, `water`, `air` | Where it drives |
| `speed` | 12 | 1 to 60 | Top speed, blocks per second |
| `acceleration` | 6 | 0.5 to 40 | How quickly it reaches top speed |
| `turn-speed` | 120 | 15 to 720 | The most it turns, degrees per second. Low is a lorry, high is a go-kart |
| `weight` | 10 | 1 to 100 | Heavier is slower to start and stop, and harder to shove |
| `scale` | 1 | 0.125 to 8 | How big the model is drawn |
| `jump` | `false` | | Space jumps instead of braking (land and water) |
| `turn-in-place` | `false` | | Can turn while standing still |
| `coast` | engine's own (a car's) | | How quickly it slows with nobody on the throttle, blocks per second squared |
| `permission` | none | | A permission needed to get in. Checked when somebody gets in |
| `capes` | `true` | | Draws riders' capes |
| `speedometer` | `true` | | Shows the driver their speed |
| `worn` | `false` | | Hides the model while somebody is in it |
| `wall-ride` | `false` | | Lets it ride up a wall |
| `animation-follows-speed` | `false` | | Ties animation speed to ground speed |
| `hitbox` | 1 × 1 × 1 | 0.25 to 16 each | `width`, `height`, `length` in blocks |

A number outside its range is clamped, with a line in the console.

`coast` matters for anything without an engine: `0.15` is a skateboard on
smooth concrete, `0.6` a bicycle, `2` a shopping trolley with a bad wheel.

## Seats

A vehicle has up to eight seats, and the order you list them is the order
people fill them. The driver always goes first wherever you write it; the rest
are passengers 1, 2, 3 in the order written.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `role` | `driver` or `passenger` (the default) |
| `x`, `y`, `z` | Right, up from the base, and forward, in blocks. They turn with the vehicle. Each is 0 if left out, and at most 8 |
| `yaw` | Turns the occupant as they sit down, degrees clockwise from the vehicle's heading. A rear-facing bench is `yaw: 180` |
| `pose` | `sitting` (default): the point is where their backside goes. `standing`: where their feet go. About a metre apart |
| `name` | What the seat is called when somebody takes it |
| `hidden` | `true` draws nobody in the seat |
| `animations` | What the occupant's body does, per state. See [What the occupant does](#what-the-occupant-does) |

```yaml
    - {role: passenger, x: 0, y: 1.2, z: -1.4, pose: standing, yaw: 180, name: "Gunner"}
    - {role: driver, y: 0.9, hidden: true}
```

A vehicle with no driver seat doesn't load, and the console names the file. If
you write two, the first drives and the rest carry passengers.

`hidden: true` is for a model with its rider built in, like an enclosed cockpit
or a tank. The occupant disappears from every screen, their own included. If
F5 shows a see-through copy of them, a scoreboard team has
`canSeeFriendlyInvisibles` on (most tab-list plugins do this). The console
names the team once.

## Bigger than three blocks

A block model can't be more than three blocks long on any axis. `scale` gets
past that: a 3-block hull at `scale: 4` is twelve blocks long. Seats and
particle emitters move with the model.

`scale` does not change the `hitbox` (so raise that yourself, or players click
a box the size of the original) or any handling number.

## Water and air

**`medium: water`** is a hull. On land it crawls at about a seventh of its top
speed, enough to get back off a beach. A vehicle that isn't a hull can drive
through a shallow ford, but deep water floods it: it wallows to a stop and
sinks.

**`medium: air`** on its own is a helicopter: it lifts straight up, holds its
height and never falls. Add a `flight:` block to make an aeroplane:

```yaml
cessna:
  model: mypack:cessna_item
  medium: air
  speed: 30
  acceleration: 8
  flight:
    takeoff-speed: 12     # won't climb below this, blocks per second (max 40)
    climb-rate: 7         # how fast space gains height (max 30)
    dive-rate: 14         # how fast S loses it (max 40)
    stall-sink: 6         # how fast it sinks when too slow (max 30)
  seats:
    - {role: driver, y: 0.9}
```

Below `takeoff-speed` the climb key does nothing, so the plane needs a runway.
Drop below it in flight and it stalls, sinking at `stall-sink` until you speed
up or land. `takeoff-speed: 0` is a helicopter again. The runway length comes
from `takeoff-speed` and `acceleration` together: 12 at 8 is about a second and
a half, roughly nine blocks. A dive keeps its speed.

A `flight:` block on a land or water vehicle does nothing, and a
`takeoff-speed` above `speed` means it can never take off. The console warns
about both.

## States

At any moment a vehicle is in one or more of these states. A car coming off a
kerb mid-corner is `airborne`, `turning` and `moving` at once.

| State | When |
|---|---|
| `airborne` | Off the ground with nothing holding it up: a jump, a fall, or an aircraft in flight |
| `turning` | Actually swinging round faster than a nudge (not just steering) |
| `reversing` | Travelling backwards |
| `moving` | Travelling forwards |
| `idle` | Stationary, or near enough |
| `submerged` | Its base is in water |

The order matters: for animations and sounds, the highest state in this table
wins.

## Animations

`animations:` maps a state to one of the model's own animations. Only one
plays at a time, picked from the highest active state. A state you leave out
falls through to the next one down, so this is a complete vehicle:

```yaml
  animations:
    idle: parked
    moving: drive
```

Cornering plays `drive`, because `turning` falls through to `moving`. Only
write `turning:` if you have drawn a lean.

- **`idle` is where the fall-through stops.** Leave it blank and a vehicle
  standing still plays nothing.
- **A moored boat is `idle`, not `submerged`.** Put its resting animation on
  `idle`. `submerged` only plays when the states above it are blank.
- An animation loops for as long as its state holds, even one authored as a
  one-shot.
- If you map both `moving` and `reversing`, draw them as the same cycle in
  opposite directions (a wheel turning 0 to 360, then 360 to 0). The engine
  carries the wheel's position across when direction changes. The two can be
  different lengths.

### A wheel should turn because the vehicle moved

By default an animation plays at the speed it was drawn, so wheels spin at the
same rate whether you're crawling or flat out. Turn this on and the ground
speed drives it instead:

```yaml
  animation-follows-speed: true
```

At top speed it plays at the drawn rate, at half speed half as fast, and
standing still it holds its frame. It's off by default because not everything
on `moving` is a wheel: a flapping flag or a bobbing suspension usually wants
its own pace.

## Sounds

`sounds:` maps the same states to [sound](/rp-engine/sounds) ids and follows
the animation rules: one at a time, highest state first, blanks fall through.
A vehicle with only a `moving` sound keeps it through corners and goes quiet
when it stops.

```yaml
  sounds:
    idle: mypack:tickover
    moving: mypack:engine
```

To loop, the sound needs a `length:` in its definition, and the engine replays
it each time it ends. Without one it plays once as the vehicle enters the
state. It plays from the vehicle and follows it. A sound id the server
doesn't have is silent rather than an error.

## What the occupant does

A seat can make its occupant's body do something per state. The values are
[emote](/rp-engine/emotes) ids:

```yaml
  seats:
    - role: driver
      y: 0.6
      animations:
        idle: mypack:lean-on-door
        moving: mypack:steering
```

Seats don't fall through like the bodywork does. A state you leave out gets
the seat's own stance (legs out for `sitting`, at rest for `standing`), so a
driver doesn't keep steering while parked. The one exception is `turning`: if
it's blank, the state underneath it is used, so the pose doesn't flicker on
every corner. An emote is worn over the stance, so one that only moves the
arms keeps the legs seated.

`submerged` never fires on a seat, because a vehicle is always `moving`,
`reversing` or `idle`. For a rowing boat, put the rest pose on `idle` and the
rowing on `moving`.

<Callout type="warning" title="When a rider shows as themselves">
  Posing a rider needs the pack to carry a rig for that player. Without one,
  or if they were already doing an emote of their own, they ride as
  themselves and the console says why, once. Set `vehicles.seat-rig: false` in
  [config.yml](/rp-engine/config#vehicles) to draw plain players in seats that
  name no emote.
</Callout>

## Rider options

- **`capes: false`** hides riders' capes in this vehicle. Use it for anything
  you sit inside (a cabin, cockpit or tank), where a cape hangs through the
  bodywork. The rider stays visible.
- **`worn: true`** hides the vehicle's model while somebody is in it, for
  things worn on the body like rollerskates or a jetpack. The seat's emotes
  carry the art as props on the rider's legs instead. Empty, it's drawn
  normally.
- **`permission:`** is checked when somebody gets in, so it works however they
  got hold of the item.

### The speedometer

The driver sees their speed above the hotbar in km/h, yellow while the tyres
are sliding. `speedometer: false` turns it off for one vehicle (a skateboard
or a horse has no dashboard), and `vehicles.speedometer: false` in
[config.yml](/rp-engine/config#vehicles) turns it off for all of them. Nothing
replaces it.

## Tricks: wall rides and bails

Both are off unless you ask, and both are for things that are ridden rather
than driven.

```yaml
  wall-ride: true
  bail:
    from: 50          # degrees off the direction of travel
    to: 130
    min-speed: 3.0    # slower than this is a stumble, not a fall
    damage: 1.0       # half a heart; 0 for none
```

**Wall ride**: jump at a wall fast and shallow enough while holding jump, and
the vehicle sticks to it, steering up and down it while you hold the key.
Letting go kicks you off.

**Bail**: land between `from` and `to` degrees off the way you were going, at
`min-speed` or faster, and the rider comes off. Landing straight, or straight
backwards, is fine. `bail: true` uses the numbers above.

## Particles

`particles:` is a list of emitters, spots on the bodywork that throw a particle
while the vehicle is in **any** of the listed states.

```yaml
  particles:
    - effect: smoke           # a particle name
      states: [moving, reversing]
      x: 0                    # same frame as a seat: right, up, forward
      y: 0.3
      z: -1.5
      count: 2                # per burst, 1 to 16 (default 1)
      interval: 2             # ticks between bursts (default 1, every tick)
      spread: 0.1             # how far they scatter, blocks (default 0)
      speed: 0.02             # how fast they drift (default 0)
      enabled: true           # false keeps it but stops it firing
    - effect: dust            # the one effect that takes a colour
      states: [idle]
      y: 1.2
      color: "#ff8800"
      size: 1
```

An emitter with no states never fires (the console says so). Old and new
particle names both work (`SMOKE_NORMAL` or `SMOKE`, `REDSTONE` or `DUST`).
Names that need a block or item (`block`, `item`, `falling_dust`) aren't
supported and are skipped with a console line. Every particle is drawn by
every player nearby, so keep `count` and `interval` modest.

## Limits and server settings

- **Vehicles collide with each other**, using their hitboxes and `weight`, so
  a lorry shunts a hatchback aside. `vehicles.collide: false` turns that off.
- **They pass through players and push mobs.** `vehicles.push-players: true`
  makes them push players too.
- **The hitbox isn't solid to the game.** It decides what you click, what stops
  the vehicle and where other vehicles hit it, but arrows, minecarts and
  players walking into it pass through.
- **Above about 20 blocks a second, passengers lag behind the vehicle** on
  their own screens. The driver is fine. Test anything faster before you ship
  it.
- **If seats sit a little off** from where the editor showed them, adjust
  `vehicles.seat-offset` (up) and `vehicles.seat-forward` (along) in
  [config.yml](/rp-engine/config#vehicles), then `/rp reload`.

<Callout type="danger" title="armor-stands-tick: false stops every vehicle">
  With that Paper setting no vehicle moves. The engine warns about it in the
  console.
</Callout>

## For plugin developers

Fuel, ownership, speed limits and no-vehicle zones are your plugin's job, and
the [Plugin API](/rp-engine/api#vehicles) covers it: who is in what, seating
and unseating people, switching a vehicle off, capping its speed, detaching
named parts, and events for getting in and out, moving, state changes, crashes
(`VehicleImpactEvent`) and bails (`VehicleBailEvent`, cancellable).
`Vehicle.wallRiding()` says whether one is on a wall right now.

Addons can keep their own settings on a vehicle under an `addons:` key. The
engine carries it through Studio syncs and `/rp edit` untouched; see the
addon's own page for what goes in it.
