# Bedrock and Geyser

[Geyser](https://geysermc.org/) lets Bedrock Edition players (phones,
consoles, Windows) join a Java server. When **Geyser-Spigot** runs on the same
server, RP Engine builds a Bedrock pack (`.mcpack`) from your content every
time it builds the Java pack, and sends it to Bedrock players as they join.
They see your custom items with your art instead of the plain vanilla item
underneath.

There is nothing to configure. The console says what went into the pack:

```
[RPEngine] Bedrock pack built: 12 items, 3 models, 4 sounds, 6 icons, 2 armour pieces.
```

It is built from your default bundle (`default-bundle` in `config.yml`) and
saved beside the Java zip in `plugins/RPEngine/output/`.

## What crosses over

- **Items with a flat sprite.** A Bedrock player sees the item with your art
  and its name. A [3D item](/rp-engine/items#3d-items) crosses only if it also
  has a sprite at its `texture:` path, which Bedrock uses as its icon: Bedrock
  cannot draw a Java model in a hand.
- **Armour** on those items, on the body, from the same equipment texture.
- **Placed models.** Each piece is drawn for Bedrock players within 48 blocks
  as a native entity, at its size and facing, with its animations playing on
  their own client. A model that uses the game's own textures gets them baked
  in. The plugin downloads them once, in the background, the first time; a
  Bedrock player who joins in those first seconds sees a checkerboard on that
  model until they next join.
- **Sounds.** A sound plays on Bedrock under the same id it plays under on
  Java, so actions and commands that play `mypack:laser` work for everyone. A
  file in `overrides/` that replaces a vanilla sound is added under the name
  Geyser plays that sound by, so it is heard on Bedrock too.
- **Icons**, in chat and anywhere else text goes. Every icon is drawn about a
  line of text tall on Bedrock, whatever its height on Java.

## What does not

**Emotes and vehicles.** Both are built from display entities, which Geyser
does not draw.

**Custom blocks.** A [custom block](/rp-engine/blocks) is a vanilla block in a
state nothing else uses, and Bedrock players see that vanilla block.

**Custom entities.** An [entity](/rp-engine/entities) is a mob made invisible
with a display entity riding it, so a Bedrock player sees neither. It can still
be hit where it stands.

**Custom screens, HUD overlays and dialogs.** All three rely on Java-only
font and dialog features.

**A handful of vanilla sounds** that Bedrock plays itself rather than by name.
Most vanilla sounds can be replaced on both editions.

## New items need a restart

Geyser learns which Java items are custom **once, at startup**. `/rp reload`
rebuilds the Bedrock pack and every Bedrock player gets the new art on their
next join, but an item that did not exist at startup shows as its base
material to Bedrock players until the server restarts. The console says so when
it happens.

## Studio models on Bedrock

A pack pushed from Studio with `/rp sync` includes a Bedrock build whenever a
player receiving it is on Bedrock, and a pack served through Studio's
distribution always has one when anything in it can cross. A Bedrock client
only accepts packs while joining, so the player is moved back onto the same
server and given the pack on the way in. It takes a few seconds, and the sync
survives it.

Placed Studio models then show up for Bedrock players as native entities, drawn
when a player comes within 48 blocks and taken away when they move off. Their
animations play on the player's own client from the pack's keyframes, started
by the same triggers as on Java.

[3D armour](/rp-engine/armour#3d-armour-from-studio) from Studio is drawn on
the body for Bedrock players too, when the pieces were handed out with
`/rp armor give` or `/rp armor wear`.

## Designing for both

If many of your players are on Bedrock, put anything they need to know in
something that crosses over, such as an item's name and lore or chat. Bedrock
players never see a menu that only exists as a custom screen, the HUD, a
dialog, an emote, a vehicle, a custom block or a custom entity.
