# Armour

Any item can be worn, with art of your own:

```yaml
crown:
  material: GOLDEN_HELMET
  model: crown
  armor: head              # head | chest | legs | feet
```

Ship one texture, and which one depends on the slot:

- `armor: legs` → `assets/textures/entity/equipment/humanoid_leggings/<id>.png`
- anything else → `assets/textures/entity/equipment/humanoid/<id>.png`

## Leggings are a different layer

Not a second one. The game draws leggings from their own narrower sheet at
their own proportions, so art drawn for the wide sheet puts a belt buckle on
somebody's knee.

That is why a slot asks for exactly one texture: declaring both layers would
name a file your pack has no reason to ship, and shipping the wrong one is a
mistake you only see on a player.

## This replaces the old tricks

<Callout type="note" title="Vanilla's own equipment path, since 1.21.4">
  An item declares an `equippable` component naming an asset, and the asset
  names the layer the game draws it from. It is the same mechanism vanilla
  armour uses.

  It replaces both of the old workarounds outright. **Dyed leather** spends a
  colour that can then never be used for anything else on the server, and looks
  wrong on every other item. **Armour trims** are stuck in the trim palette and
  limited to what the trim system can draw. Neither is needed, and neither is
  supported alongside this.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="This page needs Minecraft 1.21.4">
  Everything here is the equipment path, and that arrived in 1.21.4. On an
  older server the plugin says so at startup and:

  - **below 1.21.2**, only materials that are already armour can be worn at all,
    so `armor:` on a stick does nothing;
  - **on 1.21.2 and 1.21.3**, any item can be worn but draws with vanilla
    artwork.

  The item still works either way — it is the picture on the body that is
  missing. See [Minecraft versions](/rp-engine/versions).
</Callout>

## The model and the armour are separate

`model:` is what the item looks like **in a hand or a chest**. `armor:` is what
it looks like **on a body**. A crown can be a 3D model in your hand and a flat
texture on your head, and usually is — they are drawn by different parts of the
game and neither can substitute for the other.

An item with `armor:` and no `model:` is a vanilla-looking item that draws
custom armour, which is a perfectly ordinary thing to want.

## 3D armour from Studio

Everything above is flat: the game draws worn armour as two layers on its own
body shape, and nothing in a resource pack changes that shape. A
[3D armour set from Studio](/armor#3d-armour) is built from cubes instead, and
the engine is what puts it on a body. Sync the pack and it is there:

```
/rp armor wear studio:hazmat_suit      # put the whole set on
/rp armor give studio:hazmat_suit      # or hand over the four pieces
```

The four pieces are ordinary items. Wear them however you wear armour —
shift-click, drag into a slot, a dispenser — and take them off the same way.

### How it is drawn

- **The helmet is drawn by the game.** Any item in the head slot is rendered on
  the head from its own model, and the helmet's model is built for exactly that.
  It turns with the head frame for frame, on every client, and nobody ever sees
  their own helmet in first person.
- **Everything else is display entities**, one per piece of a limb, posed every
  other tick onto where the game is drawing that limb. The walk cycle, the body
  turning after the head, crouching, riding, the swing when you hit something,
  and an arm raised for an item, a bow or a shield are all worked out the way
  the client works them out, from the same movement.

A few things follow from that, and they are worth knowing before a player finds
them:

- **Swimming, crawling, gliding and sleeping put the body pieces away** and
  bring them back when you stand up. The game turns the whole body for those,
  and chasing it would be a second renderer for moments nobody stands still in.
- **You don't see your own body pieces by default.** In first person the game
  draws none of your body, so all you would get is a torso of armour hanging
  under the camera. `/rp armor self` shows them to you, in F5 and in first
  person, until you turn it off or leave.
- **Anyone who can't see you can't see your armour either** — a vanished
  moderator stays vanished, and so does somebody an emote has swapped for a rig.
- **A set is a handful of entities per player** wearing one — roughly ten to
  forty depending on how it was built. They are never saved and never
  collide with anything.

<Callout type="warning" title="Needs 1.21.4, and a Studio push for now">
  A set's art is named by a string `custom_model_data`, which arrived in 1.21.4;
  below that `/rp armor` says so and nothing is drawn. Sets come from a Studio
  push today — a hand-written 3D set in your content folder isn't supported yet.
</Callout>
